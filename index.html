🔒 Controlled Drug Rules (UK)

Schedule classification, prescription requirements, storage & supply rules.

Schedule Examples Prescription requirements Supply limit Storage
Sch 1
Most restricted		 LSD, psilocybin, cannabis (raw plant) Home Office licence required. Cannot be prescribed. Research only CD cabinet + Home Office approval
Sch 2
High risk		 Morphine, oxycodone, fentanyl, pethidine, methylphenidate, cocaine (topical) Handwritten (or electronic where approved): patient name/address, drug/form/strength, dose, total quantity in words AND figures, prescriber signature + date. Valid 28 days. Max 30 days' supply Locked CD cabinet; CD register required
Sch 3
High risk		 Buprenorphine, tramadol, temazepam, phenobarbital, gabapentin*, pregabalin* Same handwriting requirements as Sch 2, except temazepam/phenobarbital. Valid 28 days. Max 30 days' supply Locked CD cabinet (except gabapentinoids & temazepam in some settings)
Sch 4 Part I Benzodiazepines (except temazepam), zolpidem, zopiclone Standard FP10 prescription. No handwriting requirement. Valid 28 days. No limit specified but pharmacist discretion applies No CD cabinet required
Sch 4 Part II Anabolic steroids (nandrolone, stanozolol) Standard FP10 prescription. No set limit No CD cabinet required
Sch 5
Lowest risk		 Co-codamol 8/500, codeine linctus, kaolin & morphine Standard prescription. Some preparations OTC without prescription. No limit No special requirement

✏️ Mandatory Handwriting Requirements (Sch 2 & most Sch 3)

📋 What must be handwritten

  • Patient's full name and address
  • Date of the prescription
  • Drug name, form, strength
  • Dose instructions
  • Total quantity in words AND figures
  • Prescriber's signature (ink)
  • Prescriber's address

⏰ Validity & Repeat rules

  • Sch 2 & 3 prescriptions valid for 28 days from date
  • Sch 4 & 5 valid for 6 months
  • Sch 2 & 3 cannot be repeated — new prescription required each time
  • Instalments: prescriptions can specify daily dispensing for Sch 2 drugs (e.g. methadone)

🗝️ Safe Custody & Storage

  • Schedule 2 & 3: locked CD cabinet that complies with Misuse of Drugs (Safe Custody) Regulations 1973
  • CD register: must record receipt and supply of Sch 2 drugs; entries made at time of transaction, retained 2 years
  • Destruction of Sch 2 drugs: witnessed by an authorised person; entries in CD register

🔥 High-Yield Exam Points

  • Morphine patch: Sch 2 — requires full CD prescription
  • Tramadol: Sch 3 — needs CD script but no CD register entry
  • Gabapentin & pregabalin: Sch 3 since 2019
  • Temazepam: Sch 3 but no handwriting required
  • Methadone: usually dispensed daily; FP10MDA form for instalment prescriptions
  • "Total quantity in words AND figures" is the most commonly tested prescribing error on exams

🦠 Antibiotic Stewardship

Empirical regimens, IV-to-oral switch criteria, and stop/review reminders.

✅ IV-to-Oral Switch Criteria (any ONE met = consider switch)

  • Apyrexial for ≥24 hours
  • CRP falling or WBC normalising
  • Clinically improving (HR, BP, RR within normal range)
  • Tolerating oral fluids / no nausea or vomiting
  • GI absorption adequate (not ileus)
  • No indication for continued IV (e.g. endocarditis, CNS infection, bacteraemia)

⏰ Stop / Review Date Reminders

  • Always prescribe with a review/stop date — typically 5–7 days for community infections
  • Document indication, drug, dose, route, duration on the drug chart
  • Cultures: take before first dose where possible; review micro results at 48–72h
  • De-escalate to narrower spectrum when sensitivities known
  • Discuss prolonged courses (>14 days) with microbiology

Hormones & Contraception Navigator

Compare routine contraception, hormone replacement choices, emergency cover and post-partum considerations in one reference card.

Long-Acting Reversible (LARC)
  • Levonorgestrel IUS: 5–6 yrs, reduces menorrhagia/dysmenorrhoea, caution with distorted cavities.
  • Copper IUD: hormone-free, effective 5–10 yrs, may increase bleeding/pain.
  • Etonogestrel implant: 3 yrs, rapid return to fertility, irregular bleeding common.
  • Depo-Provera/Sayana Press: 12 weekly progestogen injection, delayed fertility return (~1 yr).
Best for those wanting “fit and forget” contraception or where oestrogen contraindicated.
Combined Hormonal Methods
  • COCP: cycle control, acne benefit. Avoid if BMI ≥35, migraine with aura, VTE risk or smoker ≥35 yrs.
  • Patch (weekly) & Vaginal ring (monthly) share COCP contraindications.
  • Requires daily/weekly adherence and counselling on missed doses & ACHES symptoms.
Assess medical eligibility (UKMEC), BP & BMI at initiation and annual review.
Missed Pill Guidance
  • COCP: 1 pill late (<24h) or missed (<48h) – take ASAP, continue pack, no extra protection. ≥2 pills missed – take last missed pill ASAP, continue pack, use condoms for 7 days and consider emergency contraception if in first week or UPSI in previous 7 days.
  • POP (desogestrel): <12h late – take ASAP, no extra precautions. ≥12h late – take ASAP, continue daily and use condoms for 48h.
  • Traditional POP (3h window): <3h late – protected; ≥3h – take ASAP and use condoms for 48h.
  • After emergency contraception: Levonorgestrel – restart pill immediately with barrier 7 days; Ulipristal – wait 5 days before restarting, then barrier 7 days.
Document reason for missed pills, pregnancy risk, and provide quick-start of reliable method if adherence is a concern.
HRT Options
  • Oestrogen-only (patch/gel/oral) for hysterectomised patients; transdermal routes lower VTE risk.
  • Combined continuous HRT when >1 yr post-menopause; sequential if peri-menopausal.
  • Micronised progesterone (body-identical) favoured for endometrial protection; consider Mirena IUS for dual use (HRT + contraception).
  • Add vaginal oestrogens for GSM symptoms irrespective of systemic regimen.
Review breast risk, VTE history, migraine and cardiovascular risk prior to prescribing. Counsel on gradual tapering when discontinuing.
Emergency Contraception
  • Copper IUD: up to 120h after UPSI or 5 days after earliest ovulation; offers ongoing contraception.
  • Ulipristal acetate 30mg: up to 120h; delay restarting hormonal contraception for 5 days and use barrier protection for 7 additional days.
  • Levonorgestrel 1.5mg: within 72h (off-label 120h with reduced efficacy); double dose if BMI >26 or weight >70kg.
  • Always perform pregnancy test if no withdrawal bleed within 3 weeks.
Check for enzyme-inducing drugs and offer bridging contraception where needed.
Post-partum Advice
  • Breastfeeding: progestogen-only methods safe immediately (POP, implant, IUS/IUD ≥4 weeks, injection ≥6 weeks).
  • Non-breastfeeding: combined hormonal methods from 21 days if low VTE risk; otherwise defer to 6 weeks.
  • Discuss quick-start of LARC before discharge, manage contraception in perinatal mental health plans.
  • Advise pelvic rest for 2 weeks after vaginal delivery and until healing after caesarean.
Assess VTE risk, breastfeeding goals, and contraception needs during routine postnatal checks.

🧒 Developmental Milestones

Track expected milestones across gross motor, fine motor, speech & language, and social-emotional domains. Use the filters to focus on a specific stream and note common presentations when skills are delayed.

Look for progression

Loss of previously gained skills or regression across any domain warrants urgent neurodevelopmental assessment.

Consider variation

Prematurity, chronic illness, and psychosocial adversity may shift timelines—correct for gestational age until 2 years.

Escalate concerns

Combine parental worries with red flags below; early referral improves outcomes for speech, motor, and social delays.

Age Domain Expected Milestone Common Presentation When Delayed
6 weeks Social Smiles responsively and fixes gaze on faces. No social smile by 8 weeks, poor eye contact, limited engagement with caregivers.
3 months Gross Motor Holds head steady in supported sitting and prone lift. Persistent head lag after 4 months, marked hypotonia or "floppy" posture.
4–5 months Fine Motor Reaches out, grasps toys, and brings hands to midline. No purposeful reaching by 5 months, asymmetric hand use, clenched fists.
6 months Speech & Language Turns to sound, babbles consonant sounds (ba/da). Silent baby with no babbling by 7 months, limited response to familiar voices.
8–9 months Gross Motor Sits without support, rolls both ways. Unable to sit by 9 months, persistent rounded back posture, only rolls one direction.
9 months Social Displays stranger anxiety, waves bye-bye, enjoys peekaboo. Indifferent to caregivers, no gestures or reciprocal play, lacks shared enjoyment.
10–12 months Fine Motor Develops neat pincer grip, transfers objects hand to hand. No pincer grasp by 12 months, persistent raking grasp, drops objects frequently.
12 months Speech & Language Says 1–2 meaningful words, responds to name, follows simple command. No single words by 15 months, poor response to name, relies on shrieks or grunts.
12–15 months Gross Motor Pulls to stand, cruises along furniture, stoops and recovers. Not weight bearing through legs, cannot pull to stand by 12 months, toe standing with tight heel cords.
18 months Gross Motor Walks independently, carries toys while walking. Not walking by 18 months, persistent bottom shuffling or toe-walking only.
18 months Speech & Language Vocabulary of 10–20 words, points to wants, follows one-step commands. Fewer than six words, absence of pointing or gesturing, frustrated tantrums from communication difficulty.
2 years Fine Motor Builds tower of six cubes, scribbles circular strokes. Cannot stack three blocks, persistent fisted grip, avoids drawing activities.
2 years Social Engages in parallel play, initiates simple pretend play. Lacks pretend play, limited joint attention, repetitive solitary behaviours.
3 years Gross Motor Pedals tricycle, jumps forward, climbs stairs alternating feet. Frequent falls, needs support on stairs, refuses to jump or pedal.
3 years Speech & Language Uses 3-word sentences, 75% intelligible to strangers. Speech largely unintelligible, echolalia only, difficulty expressing needs.
4 years Fine Motor Draws cross or person with 2–3 parts, cuts with scissors. Cannot draw circle, poor utensil use, avoids craft play due to grip difficulty.
4 years Social Cooperative play, shares and takes turns, enjoys role play. Rigid solitary play, intense difficulty sharing, aggressive outbursts in group settings.
5 years Gross Motor Balances on one foot >5 seconds, hops and skips with rhythm. Cannot hop or balance briefly, clumsy running, struggles with PE warm-ups.
5 years Speech & Language Tells simple stories, knows colours and numbers to 10. Cannot narrate events, limited vocabulary, misunderstands multi-step instructions.

Red flag triggers for urgent review

  • No smiling at 8 weeks, no babbling at 7 months, or no single words at 15 months.
  • Not sitting independently by 9 months or not walking by 18 months.
  • Hand preference before 12 months, loss of any previously acquired skill, or persistent toe walking.
  • Lack of eye contact, absence of pointing/gestures, or no pretend play by 2 years.

🪜 Clinical Treatment Ladders

💊 Corticosteroid Ladder

Glucocorticoid potency and equivalent dosing

Steroid Relative Potency Equivalent Dose Duration Clinical Uses
Cortisone 0.8x 25 mg 8-12h Rarely used (requires hepatic conversion)
Hydrocortisone 1x (baseline) 20 mg 8-12h Addison's, adrenal crisis, septic shock
Prednisolone 4x 5 mg 12-36h Asthma, COPD, polymyalgia rheumatica, IBD
Methylprednisolone 5x 4 mg 12-36h Acute asthma, COPD, spinal cord injury
Dexamethasone 25-30x 0.75 mg 36-54h Cerebral oedema, croup, severe asthma, COVID-19
Betamethasone 25-30x 0.75 mg 36-54h Fetal lung maturation, severe inflammation

🟡 Low Potency (Short-acting)

Hydrocortisone

  • Physiological replacement therapy
  • Addison's disease
  • Adrenal crisis
  • Has mineralocorticoid activity

🟠 Intermediate Potency

Prednisolone, Methylprednisolone

  • Most common for systemic use
  • Acute asthma exacerbations
  • COPD exacerbations
  • Autoimmune conditions
  • IBD flares

🔴 High Potency (Long-acting)

Dexamethasone, Betamethasone

  • Minimal mineralocorticoid activity
  • Suitable for cerebral oedema
  • Used in fetal lung maturation
  • Severe inflammatory conditions

💡 Clinical Pearls

  • Conversion: Prednisolone 40mg ≈ Hydrocortisone 100mg ≈ Dexamethasone 6mg
  • Tapering: Required if >3 weeks use, >40mg/day prednisolone, or evening doses
  • Side effects: Hyperglycaemia, immunosuppression, osteoporosis, adrenal suppression
  • Morning dosing: Mimics physiological cortisol rhythm (except dexamethasone)
  • PPI protection: Consider if high-dose, elderly, or GI risk factors

🎚️ WHO Analgesic Ladder

Stepwise approach to pain management

Step 1

🟢 Mild Pain (1-3/10)

Non-opioid ± Adjuvant

Paracetamol 1g PO/IV 6-hourly (max 4g/day) Safe, minimal side effects
Ibuprofen 400mg PO 8-hourly (max 2.4g/day) Anti-inflammatory, GI risk
Naproxen 250-500mg PO 12-hourly Longer-acting NSAID
Diclofenac 50mg PO 8-hourly Potent NSAID, CV risk
Step 2

🟠 Moderate Pain (4-6/10)

Weak Opioids ± Non-opioid ± Adjuvant

Codeine 30-60mg PO 4-6 hourly (max 240mg/day) Prodrug, 10% morphine potency
Tramadol 50-100mg PO/IV 6-hourly (max 400mg/day) Mixed mechanism, lower dependence risk
Dihydrocodeine 30mg PO 4-6 hourly Similar to codeine
Step 3

🔴 Severe Pain (7-10/10)

Strong Opioids ± Non-opioid ± Adjuvant

Morphine 5-10mg PO/2.5-5mg SC/IV 4-hourly (immediate release) Gold standard, predictable
Oxycodone 5-10mg PO 4-6 hourly 1.5x potency of morphine
Fentanyl 12-25mcg/hr patch (change 72-hourly) 100x morphine potency, for stable pain
Buprenorphine 5-20mcg/hr patch (change weekly) Partial agonist, ceiling effect

➕ Adjuvant Analgesics (All Steps)

Neuropathic Pain
  • Amitriptyline: 10-75mg nocte
  • Gabapentin: 300mg-1.2g TDS
  • Pregabalin: 75-300mg BD
  • Duloxetine: 60mg OD
Bone Pain
  • Bisphosphonates (e.g., zoledronic acid)
  • Radiotherapy for metastases
  • NSAIDs (if safe)
Muscle Spasm
  • Baclofen: 5-20mg TDS
  • Diazepam: 2-5mg BD-TDS
Inflammatory Pain
  • Corticosteroids (e.g., dexamethasone 4-8mg OD)

🔄 Opioid Conversion Guide

Medication Route Oral Morphine Equivalent
Morphine Oral 1 (baseline)
Morphine SC/IV 2-3x oral dose
Oxycodone Oral 1.5-2x
Codeine Oral ÷10
Tramadol Oral ÷10
Fentanyl patch Transdermal 12mcg/hr ≈ 30mg oral morphine/day
Buprenorphine patch Transdermal 5mcg/hr ≈ 12mg oral morphine/day

💡 Clinical Pearls

  • Breakthrough pain: 1/6th of total daily opioid dose for rescue
  • Starting strong opioids: Morphine 5mg PO 4-hourly or 2.5mg SC 4-hourly
  • Titration: Increase by 30-50% if inadequate pain control
  • Constipation: ALL patients on opioids need laxatives (senna + docusate)
  • Nausea: Common in first week, prescribe antiemetic prophylactically
  • Renal impairment: Avoid morphine, codeine (toxic metabolites); use oxycodone, fentanyl, buprenorphine
  • Hepatic impairment: Reduce doses, avoid codeine
  • By the mouth: Oral route preferred when possible
  • By the clock: Regular dosing, not PRN alone
  • Multimodal: Combine paracetamol + NSAID + opioid for additive effect
