### Q1 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Emergency Medicine

A 25-year-old male presents to the emergency department with a sudden onset of wheeze, shortness of breath, and a rash after eating at a restaurant. He has a history of peanut allergy. His blood pressure is 90/60 mmHg, heart rate is 110 bpm, and oxygen saturation is 94% on room air. 

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate immediate treatment.**

DRUG: Adrenaline (epinephrine) | Adrenaline | Epinephrine  
DOSE: 500 micrograms | 500 mcg | 0.5 mg  
ROUTE: Intramuscular (IM) | IM  
FREQUENCY: Once; repeat after 5 minutes if no improvement | PRN, q5min  
INDICATION: Anaphylaxis

> Adrenaline is the first-line treatment for anaphylaxis as it counteracts the effects of histamine, improves airway patency, and restores blood pressure through vasoconstriction. It should be administered intramuscularly into the mid-outer thigh for rapid absorption. Other treatments like antihistamines or corticosteroids are slower in onset and not immediately life-saving. Monitoring for response and side effects such as tachycardia is essential. Delayed administration could lead to worsening hypotension and airway compromise.

### Q2 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Infectious Diseases

A 68-year-old female is admitted to the hospital with suspected sepsis secondary to urinary tract infection. She is febrile with a temperature of 39°C, has a heart rate of 120 bpm, and a blood pressure of 85/50 mmHg. She is allergic to penicillin.

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate empirical treatment.**

DRUG: Gentamicin  
DOSE: 5 mg/kg (ideal body weight)  
ROUTE: Intravenous (IV) | IV  
FREQUENCY: Once daily (OD)  
INDICATION: Empirical treatment of sepsis (penicillin-allergic patient)

> In a patient with a penicillin allergy, piperacillin-tazobactam is contraindicated as it contains a penicillin ring structure and carries cross-reactivity risk. Gentamicin 5 mg/kg once daily IV is an appropriate empirical antibiotic for UTI-sourced sepsis in penicillin-allergic patients, providing coverage against common Gram-negative pathogens including E. coli and Klebsiella. Ciprofloxacin IV is an alternative, subject to local resistance patterns. Serum gentamicin levels and renal function must be monitored closely due to aminoglycoside nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity. Blood cultures should guide de-escalation once sensitivities are known.

### Q3 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Respiratory Medicine

A 19-year-old female with a history of asthma presents with an acute exacerbation. She has increased work of breathing, is using accessory muscles, and her peak flow is 40% of her personal best. She is afebrile and her oxygen saturation is 92% on air.

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate immediate treatment.**

DRUG: Salbutamol | Ventolin  
DOSE: 5 mg  
ROUTE: Nebulised | Neb  
FREQUENCY: Every 15-20 minutes, or continuously if life-threatening | q15-20min PRN  
INDICATION: Acute severe asthma exacerbation

> Salbutamol, a short-acting beta-2 agonist, is the first-line treatment for acute asthma. A peak flow of 40% of best indicates acute severe asthma (BTS classification), requiring aggressive bronchodilation. Nebulises should ideally be oxygen-driven (6-8 L/min) given the patient's SpO2 of 92%. Dosing every 15-20 minutes (or continuously in life-threatening attacks) is recommended rather than a fixed QDS schedule. Ipratropium bromide 500 micrograms nebulised can be added in acute severe or life-threatening attacks. Systemic corticosteroids (prednisolone 40-50 mg orally or hydrocortisone IV) should be given concurrently but are not immediately bronchodilating.

### Q4 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Cardiology

A 60-year-old male presents with sudden onset chest pain radiating to his left arm. He is diaphoretic and pale. His ECG shows ST-elevation in leads II, III, and aVF. He has no known drug allergies.

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate immediate treatment.**

DRUG: Aspirin  
DOSE: 300 mg | 300mg  
ROUTE: Oral (PO) | PO  
FREQUENCY: STAT | Stat | Once  
INDICATION: Acute myocardial infarction

> Aspirin is crucial in the management of acute myocardial infarction as it inhibits platelet aggregation, reducing the risk of further thrombus formation. It should be given as a loading dose immediately upon diagnosis. Alternatives like clopidogrel are used in conjunction but not as replacements for aspirin in this acute setting. Monitoring for gastrointestinal bleeding and allergy symptoms is important. Omission or delay can lead to progression of myocardial damage.

### Q5 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Respiratory Medicine

A 75-year-old male with COPD presents with increased dyspnoea, cough, and purulent sputum. He has a respiratory rate of 28, a heart rate of 100 bpm, and oxygen saturation of 88% on room air. He is on long-term inhaled tiotropium and salbutamol.

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate immediate treatment.**

DRUG: Salbutamol + Ipratropium  
DOSE: Salbutamol 2.5 mg + Ipratropium 500 micrograms  
ROUTE: Nebulised | Neb  
FREQUENCY: Every four to six hours (Q4-6H) | Q4-6H | PRN  
INDICATION: Acute COPD exacerbation

> Combining salbutamol with ipratropium, a short-acting anticholinergic, provides greater bronchodilation and symptom relief in COPD exacerbations. Nebulisation ensures effective delivery in acute settings. Oral corticosteroids like prednisolone are also indicated but are not part of immediate bronchodilator therapy. Monitoring for potential side effects such as tachycardia is essential. Inadequate treatment could result in respiratory failure requiring ventilatory support.

### Q6 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Endocrinology

A 19-year-old female with type 1 diabetes presents with vomiting, abdominal pain, and Kussmaul breathing. Her blood glucose is 30 mmol/L, and her blood gas shows a pH of 7.1. She has no known allergies.

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate immediate treatment.**

DRUG: Insulin (Actrapid)  
DOSE: 0.1 units/kg/hour | 0.1 U/kg/hr  
ROUTE: Intravenous infusion (IV) | IV  
FREQUENCY: Continuous  
INDICATION: Diabetic ketoacidosis

> Intravenous insulin is crucial for managing diabetic ketoacidosis as it reduces hyperglycaemia and ketonaemia. Actrapid is a short-acting insulin used for infusion. Continuous infusion ensures steady reduction in blood glucose and ketone levels. Frequent blood glucose and electrolyte monitoring is essential to avoid hypoglycaemia and hypokalaemia. Failure to treat promptly can lead to severe metabolic acidosis and cerebral oedema.

### Q7 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Neurology

A 50-year-old male presents with severe unilateral headache and periorbital pain. He describes it as "the worst headache of his life" and has associated lacrimation and nasal congestion. He has no significant past medical history.

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate immediate treatment.**

DRUG: Sumatriptan  
DOSE: 6 mg  
ROUTE: Subcutaneous (SC) | SC  
FREQUENCY: STAT | Stat | Once  
INDICATION: Acute cluster headache

> Sumatriptan 6 mg subcutaneous is one of two first-line treatments for acute cluster headache; the other is high-flow 100% oxygen at 10–15 L/min via a non-rebreather mask for 15–20 minutes. Both are recommended by BASH/NICE as first-line options and may be used together. Oral triptans are ineffective due to their slow onset relative to the rapid peak of a cluster attack. Monitoring for cardiovascular events is necessary due to sumatriptan's vasoconstrictive properties. Delayed treatment could prolong patient distress and disability.

### Q8 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Gastroenterology

A 55-year-old male is diagnosed with C. difficile infection following a course of broad-spectrum antibiotics. He presents with diarrhoea, abdominal pain, and fever. He is not on any current medication.

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate treatment.**

DRUG: Vancomycin  
DOSE: 125 mg  
ROUTE: Oral (PO) | PO  
FREQUENCY: Every six hours (QDS) | QDS | q6h  
DURATION: 10 days  
INDICATION: Clostridioides difficile infection

> Oral vancomycin 125 mg QDS for 10 days is first-line treatment for C. difficile infection (CDI) per NICE guidance, acting locally in the gut with negligible systemic absorption. Fidaxomicin is now the recommended second-line agent for recurrent or refractory CDI when vancomycin has failed; metronidazole is no longer recommended routinely for CDI by current NICE guidance. Monitoring for resolution of symptoms and adequate bowel function is important. Inadequate treatment could lead to severe colitis or toxic megacolon.

### Q17 | MCQ | Planning Management | Cardiology

A 64-year-old male with a history of ischemic heart disease and heart failure presents with worsening shortness of breath and peripheral oedema. He is currently taking aspirin, ramipril, bisoprolol, and simvastatin. His blood pressure is 126/78 mmHg and he has bi-basal crackles on examination. 

**What is the most appropriate medication to add to his treatment regimen?**

A. Amlodipine  
B. Furosemide ✓  
C. Spironolactone  
D. Digoxin  
E. Diltiazem

> In patients with heart failure and fluid overload, a loop diuretic such as furosemide is recommended to relieve symptoms of congestion. Amlodipine and diltiazem are not indicated as they do not address fluid overload and may worsen heart failure. Spironolactone is an option for symptomatic heart failure but is primarily for patients with NYHA class III-IV symptoms or reduced ejection fraction after optimization of other therapies. Digoxin is considered for rate control in atrial fibrillation secondary to heart failure or symptomatic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction when other treatments are inadequate.

### Q18 | MCQ | Planning Management | Endocrine

A 45-year-old woman with a history of type 2 diabetes mellitus presents with a HbA1c of 72 mmol/mol. She is currently on metformin 1 g BD and has a BMI of 32. She has no history of cardiovascular disease.

**Which medication is the most appropriate to add to her regimen?**

A. Gliclazide  
B. Sitagliptin  
C. Empagliflozin 10 mg OD ✓  
D. Pioglitazone  
E. Liraglutide

> Current NICE guidance (NG28, updated 2022) recommends considering an SGLT2 inhibitor as a first intensification step in type 2 diabetes where metformin alone is insufficient, particularly in patients who are overweight. Empagliflozin (an SGLT2 inhibitor) reduces HbA1c, aids weight loss, and provides additional cardiovascular and renal protection, making it appropriate for this patient with a BMI of 32 and inadequately controlled HbA1c. GLP-1 receptor agonists such as liraglutide (option E) are also effective and appropriate for overweight patients but current NICE pathways prioritise SGLT2 inhibitors as first add-on where tolerated. Gliclazide carries a risk of hypoglycaemia and weight gain. Pioglitazone causes fluid retention and weight gain. Sitagliptin is weight-neutral without the cardio-renal benefit profile of SGLT2 inhibitors.

### Q23 | MCQ | Planning Management | Psychiatry

A. Amitriptyline

> NICE guidance recommends that for moderate depression, pharmacological treatment should be considered alongside or following a trial of high-intensity psychological therapy. ### Q24 | MCQ | Planning Management | Renal

A 50-year-old male with chronic kidney disease (stage 3) and type 2 diabetes is found to have a blood pressure of 150/95 mmHg on two separate measurements. Urine dipstick shows 2+ protein. He is not currently taking any antihypertensive medication.

**Which medication is most appropriate to initiate?**

A. Amlodipine
B. Bendroflumethiazide
C. Lisinopril ✓
D. Losartan
E. Atenolol

### Q27 | MCQ | Providing Prescribing Decisions | Cardiology

A 75-year-old female with a history of heart failure with reduced ejection fraction is admitted with worsening peripheral oedema. She is on enalapril, bisoprolol, and furosemide.

> For worsening symptoms of heart failure with fluid overload, NICE recommends increasing diuretic dose titrated to clinical response.
