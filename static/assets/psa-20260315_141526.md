### Q1 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Emergency Medicine

A 45-year-old male presents to the A&E with acute shortness of breath, wheezing, and a history of asthma. His respiratory rate is 28/min, and he has an oxygen saturation of 90% on room air. He is using accessory muscles to breathe, and his peak flow is 150 L/min. 

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate immediate treatment.**

DRUG: Salbutamol | Ventolin
DOSE: 5 mg | 5000 micrograms | 5,000 mcg
ROUTE: Nebulised (NEB) | Neb | NEB
FREQUENCY: As required (PRN) | PRN | As needed
INDICATION: Acute exacerbation of asthma

> Salbutamol is a first-line treatment for acute asthma exacerbations, providing rapid bronchodilation. The nebulised route ensures effective delivery in severe asthma where inhaler technique may be compromised. Typical doses are 2.5-5 mg nebulised, with the higher dose used in more severe cases. Repeated doses can be given every 15-30 minutes as necessary. Other treatments, such as oral prednisolone, are used as adjuncts but not initially effective as salbutamol for immediate relief.

An 82-year-old female with a history of COPD is admitted with fever, productive cough, and confusion. Blood pressure is 95/60 mmHg, heart rate 110 bpm, and temperature 38.5°C. Blood cultures are taken, and she requires empirical antibiotic therapy for suspected sepsis.

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate immediate treatment.**

DRUG: Piperacillin-tazobactam | Tazocin
DOSE: 4.5 g | 4500 mg | 4,500 mg
ROUTE: Intravenous (IV) | IV | Intravenously
FREQUENCY: Every 8 hours (TDS) | TDS | Three times daily
INDICATION: Empirical treatment for sepsis

> Piperacillin-tazobactam is a broad-spectrum antibiotic covering a wide range of pathogens, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa, making it suitable for septic patients with potential healthcare-associated infections. This is crucial in a patient with COPD due to increased risk of resistant organisms. Alternative antibiotics may not cover such a broad spectrum or be suitable for the severity of the infection. Monitoring for renal function is essential due to potential nephrotoxicity.

### Q3 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Cardiology

A 56-year-old female presents to the A&E with severe central chest pain radiating to the left arm, lasting 45 minutes. She has a history of hypertension and hyperlipidaemia. An ECG confirms ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI).

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate immediate treatment.**

DRUG: Aspirin | Acetylsalicylic acid
DOSE: 300 mg | 300mg | 0.3 g
ROUTE: Oral (PO) | PO | By mouth
FREQUENCY: Once only (STAT) | STAT | Single dose
INDICATION: Acute myocardial infarction

> Aspirin is the cornerstone of antiplatelet therapy in acute MI, reducing mortality by preventing further thrombus formation. The oral route is rapid and effective. A 300 mg loading dose is crucial for immediate platelet inhibition. Alternatives like clopidogrel are used adjunctively but not as a replacement for the initial aspirin treatment. Monitoring for bleeding is necessary.

A 67-year-old male known with COPD presents with increased sputum production, dyspnoea, and wheezing over the last 2 days. He has a respiratory rate of 24/min and an oxygen saturation of 88% on room air.

ROUTE: Nebulised (NEB) | Neb | NEB

### Q5 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Endocrinology

A 23-year-old female with known type 1 diabetes mellitus presents with nausea, vomiting, and polyuria. Her blood glucose is 28 mmol/L, ketones are high, and venous blood gas shows a pH of 7.25.

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate immediate treatment.**

ROUTE: Intravenous infusion (IV) | IV | Intravenously
FREQUENCY: Continuous infusion | Continuous | Infusion

### Q6 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Haematology

A 62-year-old male with a body weight of 80 kg presents with sudden onset of pleuritic chest pain and dyspnoea. A CT pulmonary angiogram confirms a pulmonary embolism.

**Complete a prescription for the most appropriate immediate treatment.**

DRUG: Enoxaparin | Clexane
DOSE: 80 mg | 1 mg/kg | 80mg
ROUTE: Subcutaneous (SC) | SC | Subcut
FREQUENCY: Every 12 hours (BD) | BD | Twice daily
INDICATION: Pulmonary embolism

> Enoxaparin, a low molecular weight heparin, is effective for initial anticoagulation in pulmonary embolism. The dosing is weight-based (1 mg/kg BD), allowing for predictable anticoagulant effects without routine monitoring. This is preferred over unfractionated heparin due to ease of administration and lower risk of HIT. Warfarin would be used for long-term anticoagulation but is not suitable for immediate anticoagulation.

### Q8 | PRESCRIPTION | Prescription Writing | Neurology

DOSE: 6 mg | 6mg | 0.006 g

Oxygen therapy can be adjunctively helpful but does not replace the efficacy of sumatriptan SC in aborting attacks.

**Current prescription:**
| Drug | Dose | Route | Frequency | Indication |
|------|------|-------|-----------|------------|
| Ramipril | 10 mg | Oral | OD | Hypertension |
| Ibuprofen | 400 mg | Oral | TDS | Pain |

**Part A: What is the problem with this prescription?**

A. Incorrect dosing of ramipril
B. Risk of interaction between ibuprofen and ramipril ✓
C. Ibuprofen dose too high
D. Ramipril is contraindicated
E. Wrong indication for ibuprofen

**Part B: What action should you take?**

A. Discontinue ibuprofen ✓
B. Increase ramipril dose
C. Add furosemide
D. Replace ramipril with losartan
E. Reduce ibuprofen dose

The appropriate action is to discontinue ibuprofen and consider alternative analgesics that do not affect renal function or interact with the patient's other medications.

> According to NICE guidelines, beta-blockers such as atenolol are considered first-line treatment for stable angina unless contraindicated.
