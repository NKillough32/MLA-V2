### Q1 | PRESCRIBING | Prescribing | Gynaecology

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 54-year-old woman attends her GP with a 2-year history of vaginal dryness and dyspareunia. Her last menstrual period was 2 years ago. She has no hot flushes, no night sweats, and no other systemic menopausal symptoms. She tells her GP she would like a local treatment only and does not wish to use systemic hormones. Her cervical screening is up to date. **PMH.** Hypertension. **DH.** Amlodipine 5 mg PO daily. No known drug allergies. Uterus intact. No personal or first-degree family history of breast cancer or VTE.

**On examination**
BP 134/84 mmHg. Inspection confirms vulvovaginal atrophy with pale, dry vaginal mucosa and reduced vaginal rugae.

**Investigations**
FSH 62 IU/L (> 30 confirms menopause). Cervical smear negative.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE preparation that is *most appropriate* to treat this patient's vaginal symptoms.
*(use the 'regular medicines' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**
> Local vaginal oestrogen is the first-line treatment for isolated vulvovaginal atrophy (genitourinary syndrome of menopause). Low-dose local preparations — such as estradiol vaginal pessaries 10 micrograms (Vagifem) — produce effective local relief with negligible systemic absorption. The BNF and NICE NG23 state that systemic progestogen is NOT required alongside low-dose vaginal oestrogen preparations, even in women with an intact uterus, as the systemic absorption is insufficient to stimulate the endometrium. Estriol 0.1% vaginal cream (Ovestin) and the estradiol vaginal ring are equally appropriate alternatives. Systemic HRT is unnecessary — and inappropriate to prescribe — when symptoms are limited to the vagina.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> Estradiol 10 micrograms vaginal pessary inserted vaginally once daily for 14 days (initial treatment phase), then twice weekly as maintenance. No specific time of day is required. Applicator-free insertion is described in the patient information leaflet.

**Optimal answers**

DRUG_OPTION: estradiol 10 micrograms vaginal pessaries
  DOSE: 1 pessary vaginally once daily for 14 days then twice weekly [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 pessary vaginally once daily for 2 weeks then twice weekly [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 pessary vaginally once daily for 14 days then 2 times a week [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 pessary vaginally daily for 14 days then twice weekly [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 pessary vaginally once daily [5/10]
  DOSE: 1 pessary vaginally twice weekly [5/10]

DRUG_OPTION: estriol 0.1% vaginal cream (Ovestin)
  DOSE: 0.5 g vaginally once daily for 3 weeks then twice weekly [10/10]
  DOSE: 0.5 g vaginally daily for 3 weeks then twice weekly [10/10]
  DOSE: 0.5 g applied vaginally once daily [5/10]

DRUG_OPTION: estradiol 2 mg vaginal ring (Estring)
  DOSE: 1 ring inserted vaginally, replace every 90 days [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 ring vaginally every 3 months [10/10]

### Q2 | PRESCRIBING | Prescribing | Gynaecology

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 61-year-old woman presents to her GP with recurrent episodes of bacterial vaginosis and three urinary tract infections in the past 12 months, confirmed on urine cultures. She also describes vaginal dryness and mild dyspareunia during intercourse. She denies hot flushes or other systemic menopausal symptoms. She underwent a total abdominal hysterectomy 9 years ago for uterine fibroids; her ovaries were conserved. She now appears to be postmenopausal based on symptoms. **PMH.** Hysterectomy (fibroids), osteoarthritis. **DH.** Paracetamol 500 mg PO as required, ibuprofen 400 mg PO as required.

**On examination**
Vaginal atrophy confirmed on speculum examination. Bimanual examination: no adnexal mass.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE preparation most appropriate to treat her genitourinary symptoms.
*(use the 'regular medicines' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**
> Local (topical) vaginal oestrogen is the most appropriate treatment for genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) with recurrent UTIs. Evidence shows that topical oestrogen significantly reduces the rate of recurrent lower UTIs in postmenopausal women by restoring the vaginal lactobacillus flora, lowering vaginal pH, and improving urethral mucosal integrity. Because this patient had a hysterectomy, there is no endometrium to protect — progestogen is not needed, whether using local or systemic therapy. Systemic HRT is unnecessary and overtreatment for isolated vaginal/urinary symptoms.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> Estradiol 10 micrograms pessary once daily for 14 days, then twice weekly as maintenance. Evaluate response at 3 months and continue as needed. Recurrent UTIs: local oestrogen may take 6–8 weeks to reduce UTI frequency noticeably.

**Optimal answers**

DRUG_OPTION: estradiol 10 micrograms vaginal pessaries
  DOSE: 1 pessary vaginally once daily for 14 days then twice weekly [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 pessary vaginally once daily for 2 weeks then twice weekly [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 pessary vaginally once daily [5/10]
  DOSE: 1 pessary vaginally twice weekly [5/10]

DRUG_OPTION: estriol 0.1% vaginal cream (Ovestin)
  DOSE: 0.5 g vaginally once daily for 3 weeks then twice weekly [10/10]
  DOSE: 0.5 g applied vaginally daily [5/10]

A 57-year-old woman with an intact uterus attends her GP requesting help for vaginal dryness and urgency urinary incontinence. Her last menstrual period was 4 years ago. She has mild vaginal symptoms only — no systemic vasomotor symptoms. She is reluctant to use any form of HRT, citing a strong maternal family history of breast cancer, and would like the lowest possible hormonal exposure. **PMH.** Type 2 diabetes, mild stress urinary incontinence. **DH.** Metformin hydrochloride 500 mg PO twice daily.

**On examination**
BP 128/78 mmHg. Examination confirms vulvovaginal atrophy and urethral caruncle.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE hormonal preparation appropriate for her vaginal symptoms, consistent with her wish to minimise systemic hormonal exposure.
*(use the 'regular medicines' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**

Estriol cream is particularly well suited here: estriol is a weak oestrogen with very low systemic activity, and topical estriol at recommended doses shows no measureable increase in endometrial proliferation or breast exposure in studies. Systemic progestogen is not required alongside low-dose local oestrogen preparations even with an intact uterus. Vaginal estradiol pessaries are equally appropriate.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> Estriol 0.1% vaginal cream 0.5 g (via applicator) intravaginally once daily for 3 weeks, then twice weekly as long-term maintenance. Review annually. No additional progestogen necessary.

### Q4 | PRESCRIBING | Prescribing | Gynaecology

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 48-year-old woman with an intact uterus presents to her GP with a 6-month history of troublesome hot flushes, night sweats and mood disturbance. Her periods have become irregular over the past 18 months and her last menstrual period was 6 months ago. She is not sexually active. She has no contraindications to HRT and is happy to continue having monthly withdrawal bleeds. She prefers to take tablets. **PMH.** Nil significant. **DH.** Nil. No known drug allergies.

**On examination**
BP 122/76 mmHg, BMI 23.

**Investigations**
TSH 1.8 mU/L (0.5–5.5). FSH 38 IU/L (elevated for her age, consistent with perimenopause — not used diagnostically in women <45 years on its own, but noted).

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE HRT preparation most appropriate for this perimenopausal woman with an intact uterus.
*(use the 'regular medicines' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**
> Sequential (cyclical) combined HRT is the correct choice. Because this woman is perimenopausal (LMP < 1 year, still likely to be having irregular periods), she needs cyclical progestogen to: (a) protect the endometrium from oestrogen-induced hyperplasia, and (b) produce a regular monthly withdrawal bleed (which reassures her that abnormal bleeding is not occurring). Continuous combined HRT is CONTRAINDICATED here — initiating it in the perimenopause causes irregular, unpredictable breakthrough bleeding. Oestrogen alone is NEVER appropriate with an intact uterus (endometrial cancer risk). An oral sequential combined preparation containing oestradiol with cyclical dydrogesterone or norethisterone is appropriate.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> Estradiol 2 mg / dydrogesterone 10 mg sequential tablets — 1 tablet orally daily (the pack provides 14 tablets of estradiol 2 mg alone, followed by 14 tablets of estradiol 2 mg + dydrogesterone 10 mg). Taken continuously with no break between packs.

**Optimal answers**

DRUG_OPTION: estradiol 2 mg/dydrogesterone 10 mg sequential tablets (Femoston 2/10)
  DOSE: 1 tablet orally once daily [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 tablet orally daily [10/10]

DRUG_OPTION: estradiol 1 mg/dydrogesterone 10 mg sequential tablets (Femoston 1/10)
  DOSE: 1 tablet orally once daily [8/10]
  DOSE: 1 tablet orally daily [8/10]

DRUG_OPTION: estradiol 2 mg/norethisterone acetate 1 mg sequential tablets (Elleste Duet 2 mg)
  DOSE: 1 tablet orally once daily [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 tablet orally daily [10/10]

DRUG_OPTION: estradiol 1 mg/norethisterone acetate 1 mg sequential tablets (Elleste Duet 1 mg)
  DOSE: 1 tablet orally once daily [8/10]

### Q5 | PRESCRIBING | Prescribing | Gynaecology

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 49-year-old woman attends her GP with troublesome vasomotor symptoms (hot flushes and night sweats) that are significantly affecting her sleep and quality of life. Her last menstrual period was 9 months ago; her periods have been irregular for 2 years. Uterus intact. She has no contraindications to HRT. She dislikes swallowing tablets and would prefer a transdermal option. She is happy to have a monthly withdrawal bleed. **PMH.** Nil. **DH.** Nil. No known drug allergies.

**On examination**
BP 118/72 mmHg, BMI 25.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE HRT preparation most appropriate for this perimenopausal woman who prefers a transdermal preparation.
*(use the 'regular medicines' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**
> A sequential combined transdermal patch is the correct choice. The patient is perimenopausal (LMP 9 months ago) with an intact uterus — sequential progestogen is required. Transdermal delivery bypasses hepatic first-pass metabolism, conferring a lower VTE risk compared with oral HRT preparations. Sequential combined patches (e.g., Evorel Sequi) provide two alternating patch types within each 28-day cycle: an oestradiol-only patch (phase 1: days 1–14) and an oestradiol plus norethisterone patch (phase 2: days 15–28). Each patch is applied twice weekly (changed every 3–4 days). A monthly withdrawal bleed typically occurs during or shortly after the phase 2 patches are applied.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> Estradiol/norethisterone sequential transdermal patches (Evorel Sequi): apply one patch to clean, dry, non-hairy skin (e.g., buttock, abdomen, outer thigh — NOT breasts) twice weekly, changing every 3–4 days. Follow the pack sequence: 8 phase-1 (oestradiol only) patches, then 8 phase-2 (combined) patches, per 28-day cycle. Start on day 1 of a spontaneous bleed or at any point if amenorrhoeic.

**Optimal answers**

DRUG_OPTION: estradiol/norethisterone sequential transdermal patches (Evorel Sequi)
  DOSE: 1 patch applied to skin twice weekly, changing every 3 to 4 days [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 patch applied to skin twice weekly [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 patch to non-hairy skin twice weekly, following pack sequence [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 patch every 3 to 4 days [10/10]

### Q6 | PRESCRIBING | Prescribing | Gynaecology

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 46-year-old woman presents to her GP requesting help with vasomotor menopausal symptoms. Her periods have become irregular over the past year and her last menstrual period was 4 months ago. She is sexually active and specifically requests ongoing contraception. She is a non-smoker, normotensive, BMI 23, and has no personal history of VTE, migraine, cardiovascular disease, or breast cancer. She does not have migraine with or without aura. **PMH.** Nil. **DH.** Nil. No known drug allergies. Uterus intact. UKMEC category 1 for all combined hormonal contraception criteria.

**On examination**
BP 116/72 mmHg.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE preparation that can provide *both* menopausal symptom management *and* contraception for this perimenopausal woman.
*(use the 'regular medicines' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**
> Estradiol valerate/dienogest tablets (Qlaira) is a combined oral contraceptive (COC) uniquely formulated with oestradiol valerate (not ethinylestradiol) and a progestogen, making it the only licensed COC that also provides symptomatic menopausal benefit in perimenopause — it is sometimes referred to as a 'combined' or 'natural' oral contraceptive with HRT-like oestrogen. Standard COC rules apply fully (UKMEC must be applied). Because this patient is perimenopausal and needs contraception, this is the correct combined prescription. Migraine with aura is a UKMEC 4 absolute contraindication to all combined hormonal contraception — this patient does NOT have migraine with aura, so Qlaira is appropriate. When periods stop for >1 year AND the patient is > 50 years, contraception can be stopped; she should then transition to HRT if still symptomatic.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> Estradiol valerate/dienogest tablets (Qlaira): 1 tablet orally daily without a break, starting on day 1 of a spontaneous menstrual bleed for immediate contraceptive cover. Follow the 28-tablet pack sequence (tablets are colour-coded by phase). No pill-free interval.

**Optimal answers**

DRUG_OPTION: estradiol valerate/dienogest tablets (Qlaira)
  DOSE: 1 tablet orally once daily without a break, starting day 1 of period [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 tablet orally daily, following pack instructions [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 tablet orally daily without break [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 tablet orally once daily [8/10]
  DOSE: 1 tablet orally daily [8/10]

**Case presentation**
A 47-year-old woman with an intact uterus presents to her GP with distressing hot flushes and night sweats. Her last menstrual period was 7 months ago. She reports that her migraines have worsened since her periods became irregular. On further questioning, her migraines are always preceded by visual zigzag lines lasting 20 minutes — consistent with migraine with aura. She is not on any current contraception. She has no other contraindications to HRT. **PMH.** Migraine with aura (diagnosed age 32), occasional tension headache. **DH.** Sumatriptan 50 mg PO as required.

**On examination**
BP 126/78 mmHg, BMI 24. Neurological examination normal.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for the *oestrogen component* of sequential HRT most appropriate for this woman.
*(A progestogen will be prescribed separately. Use the 'regular medicines' prescription form provided.)*

**Drug choice**
> Combined oral contraceptives (COC) are UKMEC category 4 (absolute contraindication) in migraine with aura due to a clinically significant increase in ischaemic stroke risk. This means Qlaira (estradiol valerate/dienogest COC) is absolutely contraindicated. However, transdermal HRT (patches or gel) is NOT contraindicated in women with migraine with aura — the very low oestradiol dose used in HRT does not suppress ovulation, carries no meaningful VTE or stroke risk elevation, and NICE NG23 explicitly permits its use. Transdermal oestradiol (patch) is therefore the correct oestrogen component of sequential HRT for this perimenopausal woman. A progestogen (e.g., norethisterone acetate or micronised progesterone) must be prescribed separately to protect the endometrium.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> Estradiol 50 micrograms/24 hours transdermal patch applied to clean, dry, non-hairy skin (buttock or abdomen) twice weekly (changed every 3–4 days). Do NOT apply to breasts. A progestogen must be prescribed separately for endometrial protection (e.g., norethisterone acetate 5 mg PO on days 15–26 of each cycle or micronised progesterone 200 mg PO for 12 days per cycle).

**On examination**
BP 128/80 mmHg, BMI 25.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE HRT preparation most appropriate for this postmenopausal woman with an intact uterus who prefers oral tablets.
*(use the 'regular medicines' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**

**Case presentation**
A 55-year-old woman with an intact uterus consults her GP requesting treatment for persistent hot flushes and night sweats. Her last menstrual period was 3 years ago. She has a history of an unprovoked deep vein thrombosis (DVT) 4 years ago, for which she was fully anticoagulated for 6 months; anticoagulation was subsequently stopped, and she has had no further thrombotic events. BMI 32. She does not have thrombophilia on testing. **PMH.** Unprovoked DVT (resolved). **DH.** Nil currently.

**On examination**
BP 136/84 mmHg, BMI 32.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for the *oestrogen component* of the most appropriate HRT regimen for this postmenopausal woman with a history of DVT.
*(Progestogen will be prescribed separately to protect the endometrium. Use the 'regular medicines' prescription form provided.)*

> Oral HRT increases VTE risk via hepatic first-pass metabolism (oestrogen stimulates hepatic synthesis of procoagulant factors; hazard ratio for VTE with oral HRT approximately 1.9–2.0). In a woman with a history of unprovoked DVT, the BNF and NICE NG23 recommend transdermal oestradiol as the preferred route. A progestogen must still be prescribed separately (uterus intact). Micronised progesterone (Utrogestan) and norethisterone acetate are options; some evidence suggests micronised progesterone may be safer in terms of VTE risk than norethisterone.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> Estradiol 50 micrograms/24 hours transdermal patch applied to non-hairy skin twice weekly (changing every 3–4 days). Progestogen to be prescribed separately (e.g., micronised progesterone 200 mg PO daily for 12 days per month as sequential, OR 100 mg PO continuously for no-bleed regimen). Ideally managed with haematology/menopause specialist input.

**Optimal answers**

DRUG_OPTION: estradiol 50 micrograms/24 hours transdermal patches
  DOSE: 1 patch applied to skin twice weekly [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 patch to non-hairy skin twice weekly, changing every 3 to 4 days [10/10]
  DOSE: 1 patch every 3 to 4 days [10/10]

A 56-year-old woman attends her GP requesting HRT for hot flushes and night sweats. Her last menstrual period was 4 years ago. Uterus intact. She has no contraindications to HRT. She dislikes tablets and would prefer a transdermal preparation. She is happy not to have any bleeds. **PMH.** Nil. **DH.** Nil. No known drug allergies.

**On examination**

Write a prescription for ONE HRT preparation most appropriate for this postmenopausal woman who prefers a transdermal patch and does not want withdrawal bleeds.
