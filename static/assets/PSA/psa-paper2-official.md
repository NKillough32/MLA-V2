### Q1 | PRESCRIBING | Prescribing | Surgery

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 55-year-old woman on the gastroenterology ward had an elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy 2 hours ago. She feels nauseated and has vomited twice. **PMH.** Obesity, type 2 diabetes, bipolar disorder. **DH.** Ramipril 5 mg PO daily, gliclazide 80 mg PO daily, metformin hydrochloride 1 g PO twice daily, quetiapine 300 mg PO 12-hrly. She was given morphine 5 mg IV and dexamethasone 3.3 mg IV in the recovery room and post operative IV fluid replacement is in progress.

**On examination**
Temperature 36.5°C, HR 98/min and rhythm regular, BP 112/90 mmHg, RR 20/min, O_2 sat 97% breathing air.

**Investigations**
Na^+ 142 mmol/L (137–144), K^+ 3.4 mmol/L (3.5–5.3), U 6.5 mmol/L (2.5–7.0), Cr 95 µmol/L (60–110), 'finger prick' capillary glucose 8.3 mmol/L.
QTc 485 ms (<460).

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE drug that is *most appropriate* to treat her nausea and vomiting.
(*use the 'once-only medicines' prescription form provided*)

**Drug choice**
> The BNF treatment summary for nausea and labyrinth disorders has a section on post-operative nausea and vomiting (PONV). It suggests that two or more drugs should be used, and when one has failed, another from a different class can be tried.
> In this patient, who has already received dexamethasone, an alternative agent is indicated. As they have a prolonged QT interval, and are taking an antipsychotic, some drugs are unsuitable. As she is vomiting, the oral route is impractical.
> Cyclizine is the drug of choice in this patient.  It is available via the IV route, is licensed for this indication, and has no interactions, cautions or contraindications in this scenario.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> The correct dose is 50 mg IM or IV. All other doses and routes score zero.

**Optimal answers**

DRUG_OPTION: cyclizine 50 mg/mL injection
  DOSE: 50 mg intramuscular three times daily
  DOSE: 50 mg intravenous three times daily
  DOSE: 1 mL intramuscular daily
  DOSE: 1 mL intravenous once only

### Q4 | PRESCRIBING | Prescribing | Endocrinology

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 45-year-old woman is referred to the primary care diabetes clinic because of poor blood glucose control. She has no significant symptoms of hyperglycaemia.** PMH.** Hypertension, hypercholesterolaemia. **DH.** Ramipril 5 mg PO daily, amlodipine 5 mg PO daily.** FH. **Father and elder sister have type 2 diabetes. **SH. **Non-smoker.

**On examination
**BP 126/70 mmHg, BMI 30 kg/m^2 (18-25).

**Investigations
**Na^+ 139 mmol/L (137–144), K^+ 4.5 mmol/L (3.5–5.3), U 6.3 mmol/L (2.5–7.0), Cr 69 µmol/L (60–110), eGFR 80 mL/min/1.73 m^2 (>60).
HbA_1c 75 mmol/mol (20–42).

**Prescribing request**
*Write a prescription for ONE drug that is *most appropriate* to improve her blood glucose control.
(*use the 'general practice' prescription form provided*)

**Drug choice**
> Standard-release metformin hydrochloride is a recommended first-line treatment for type 2 diabetes mellitus, with favourable effects on weight, long-term cardiovascular benefits, and a low risk of hypoglycaemia.
> The mark allocation for this practice question is based on advice in the BNF and approved by the assessment board in November 2025. At that time, the BNF advised initial treatment with a standard-release formulation of metformin. This should be started at a low dose and increased gradually to minimise gastro-intestinal side effects. Under this guidance, an appropriate initial prescription would be metformin hydrochloride 500 mg PO daily.
> In February 2026, new NICE guidance recommended that patients with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes without comorbidities should be started on modified-release metformin and an SGLT-2 inhibitor (e.g. dapagliflozin).
> Candidates are reminded to use the BNF when answering PSA questions. In a summative assessment, full credit will be awarded for any prescription that, in the judgement of the assessment board, reflects optimal practice according to BNF guidance available at the time. However, where guidance has changed, candidates will not be disadvantaged for demonstrating awareness of newer guidance.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> The recommended starting dose for at least one week is 500 mg PO daily. Start low to minimise side effects, so higher doses score zero. Liquid preparations are less convenient (1 mark deducted). All other doses score zero.

**Optimal answers**

  DOSE: 1 capsule oral daily
  DOSE: 500 mg oral daily
  DOSE: 1 tablet oral daily
  DOSE: 500 mg oral nightly
  DOSE: 1 tablet oral nightly

DRUG_OPTION: metformin hydrochloride 500 mg m/r tablets
  DOSE: 500 mg oral daily
  DOSE: 1 tablet oral daily
  DOSE: 500 mg oral nightly
