### Q1 | PRESCRIBING | Prescribing | Respiratory

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 72-year-old man presents to the medical assessment unit with shortness of breath and oedema for 1 week. **PMH.** Ischaemic heart disease, type 2 diabetes, CKD, leg ulcers, gout. **DH.** Biphasic isophane insulin (Humulin M3^®) 16 units SC 12-hrly, linagliptin 5 mg PO daily, bisoprolol fumarate 2.5 mg PO daily, alfacalcidol 500 micrograms PO daily, allopurinol 100 mg PO daily, clopidogrel 75 mg PO daily, ramipril 5 mg PO daily, isosorbide mononitrate m/r 60 mg PO daily, nicorandil 20 mg PO 12-hrly.

**On examination**
Temperature 37.1°C, HR 78/min and rhythm regular, BP 148/90 mmHg, JVP raised, RR 22/min, O_2 sat 94% breathing 40% oxygen. Bilateral pitting leg oedema to thighs.

**Investigations**
Na^+ 138 mmol/L (137–144), K^+ 5.4 mmol/L (3.5–5.3), U 15.1 mmol/L (2.5–7.0), Cr 187 µmol/L (60–110).

CXR shows cardiomegaly and pulmonary oedema.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE drug that is *most appropriate* to alleviate his breathlessness and oedema.
(*use the ‘once-only medicines’ prescription form provided*)

**Drug choice**
> This patient has pulmonary oedema which requires immediate treatment. The BNF (Diuretics monograph) states 'loop diuretics are used in pulmonary oedema due to left ventricular failure; intravenous administration produces relief of breathlessness and reduces pre-load sooner than would be expected from the time of onset of diuresis. Loop diuretics are also used in patients with chronic heart failure.' Furosemide is the optimal choice as it can be given IV, with a rapid-onset of action. Full 'drug choice' credit (5 marks) is given for selecting furosemide. Formulation/dose/route credit (up to 5 further marks) is given for selecting injectable formulations of furosemide, given IV and at a suitable dose. IM administration would be less effective, and the oral route would be too slow.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> The correct dose is 20-50 mg IV as a once only dose. Doses below 20 mg score zero. Doses above 50 mg up to 100 mg would be effective but unnecessarily high (deduct 1 mark).

### Q9 | MCQ | Planning | General Medicine

MARKS: 2

**Case presentation**
**Case presentation
**A 72-year-old woman presents to her GP with a history of a few days of shivering and myalgia combined with mild burning pain and numbness of the skin on the the left side of her chest. This culminated in the development of a rash in the affected area one day previously. **PMH.** Total left hip replacement, inflammatory bowel disease. **D****H.** Azathioprine 100 mg PO daily.

**On examination**
Temperature 37.3°C. There is an erythematous vesicular rash consistent with involvement of the T7 dermatome on the left.

She is advised to take aciclovir 800 mg PO five times daily.

**Question**
Select the *most appropriate* management option for her pain at this stage.
(*mark it with a tick*)

A. amitriptyline hydrochloride 10 mg PO nightly
B. co-codamol 30/500 two tablets PO 6-hrly
C. gabapentin 300 mg PO daily
D. ibuprofen 400 mg PO 8-hrly
E. paracetamol 1 g PO 6-hrly ✓

> **A:** Amitriptyline hydrochloride is a useful treatment for postherpetic neuralgia (unlicensed indication) unresponsive to other simpler measures, but it is not the first-choice treatment in an acute episode because of its various anticholinergic adverse effects (e.g. dry mouth, blurred vision, tachycardia, urinary retention).
> **B:** Although paracetamol is a good initial choice, the combination with relatively high doses of codeine adds considerably to the risk of adverse effects, and these additional risks are not justified by any subsequent clinical benefit. The combined treatment may be justified if paracetamol alone is ineffective.
> **C:** Gabapentin is a useful treatment for postherpetic neuralgia unresponsive to other simpler measures, but it is not the first-choice treatment in an acute episode because of its various adverse effects (e.g. drowsiness).
> **D:** Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) carry some risks in more elderly patients, notably those of gastrotoxicity. NSAIDs should also be used with caution in Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis, as these conditions may be exacerbated.
> **E:** Paracetamol is the simplest initial approach to treatment of pain in an elderly person who might be vulnerable to the adverse effects of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (e.g. gastrotoxicity, renal impairment) and opioid analgesics (e.g. constipation, nausea, drowsiness). The pain is described as mild, so there is no indication that stronger analgesia is indicated.
