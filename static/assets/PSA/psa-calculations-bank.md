### Q1 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Paediatrics

An 18 kg child with a lower respiratory tract infection is prescribed oral amoxicillin 25 mg/kg per dose three times daily. The suspension available is amoxicillin 125 mg/5 mL.

**Calculate the volume of suspension to administer per dose, in mL.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 18
TOLERANCE: 0.1
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the dose per administration = 18 kg × 25 mg/kg = 450 mg.
Step 2: Calculate the volume = (450 mg ÷ 125 mg) × 5 mL = **18 mL**.

> Amoxicillin is first-line for community-acquired pneumonia and acute otitis media in children. Always calculate dose per kg and verify against the maximum allowed dose. The suspension strength varies between 125 mg/5 mL, 250 mg/5 mL, and 500 mg/5 mL — confirm before dispensing.

### Q2 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Emergency Medicine

A 72 kg adult presents in acute severe pain following trauma. The team prescribes IV morphine sulfate 0.1 mg/kg as a titrated bolus dose. The ampoule available contains morphine sulfate 10 mg/mL.

**Calculate the volume of morphine sulfate solution to draw up, in mL.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 0.72
TOLERANCE: 0.02
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the dose = 72 kg × 0.1 mg/kg = 7.2 mg.
Step 2: Calculate the volume = 7.2 mg ÷ 10 mg/mL = **0.72 mL**.

> IV morphine for acute pain should be titrated in small increments (typically 1–2 mg IV every 5 minutes) rather than given as a single large bolus. Have naloxone immediately available. Dose reduction is required in renal or hepatic impairment, and in the elderly.

### Q3 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Infectious Disease

A patient is prescribed piperacillin/tazobactam 2.25 g IV. The available vials contain piperacillin/tazobactam 4.5 g, which are reconstituted with 20 mL sodium chloride 0.9% to give a solution of 225 mg/mL.

**Calculate the volume of reconstituted solution to draw up for each dose, in mL.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 10
TOLERANCE: 0.5
WORKING:
Step 1: Confirm the concentration after reconstitution = 4500 mg ÷ 20 mL = 225 mg/mL.
Step 2: Calculate the volume required = 2250 mg ÷ 225 mg/mL = **10 mL**.

> Piperacillin/tazobactam is a broad-spectrum beta-lactam/beta-lactamase inhibitor combination. After reconstitution it must be further diluted in 50–150 mL of compatible fluid prior to infusion over 30 minutes. Dose adjustment is required if eGFR < 40 mL/min/1.73 m².

### Q4 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Cardiology

An 85 kg patient with pulmonary embolism is commenced on a weight-based heparin infusion at 18 units/kg/hour. The infusion bag contains heparin 25,000 units in 50 mL sodium chloride 0.9%.

**Calculate the infusion rate in mL/hour. Give your answer to 2 decimal places.**

UNIT: mL/hour
ANSWER: 3.06
TOLERANCE: 0.05
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the hourly dose = 85 kg × 18 units/kg/h = 1530 units/h.
Step 2: Calculate the concentration = 25,000 units ÷ 50 mL = 500 units/mL.
Step 3: Calculate the rate = 1530 units/h ÷ 500 units/mL = **3.06 mL/h**.

> Standard heparin infusion for VTE: loading dose 80 units/kg IV, then 18 units/kg/h. Monitor APTT 6 hours after starting (target 1.5–2.5× control) and after each dose adjustment. Check platelets every 2–3 days from day 4 to 14 to detect heparin-induced thrombocytopaenia (HIT).

### Q5 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Critical Care

A 60 kg patient with cardiogenic shock is commenced on dopamine at 4 micrograms/kg/minute. The infusion available is dopamine 400 mg in 250 mL glucose 5%.

**Calculate the infusion rate in mL/hour.**

UNIT: mL/hour
ANSWER: 9
TOLERANCE: 0.2
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the dose per minute = 60 kg × 4 mcg/kg/min = 240 mcg/min.
Step 2: Convert to hourly dose = 240 mcg/min × 60 min/h = 14,400 mcg/h = 14.4 mg/h.
Step 3: Calculate the concentration = 400 mg ÷ 250 mL = 1.6 mg/mL.
Step 4: Calculate the rate = 14.4 mg/h ÷ 1.6 mg/mL = **9 mL/h**.

> Dopamine has dose-dependent effects: at 2–5 mcg/kg/min it acts on dopaminergic receptors (renal vasodilation); at 5–10 mcg/kg/min beta-1 effects predominate (increased cardiac output); above 10 mcg/kg/min alpha-1 effects cause vasoconstriction. It is administered via a central venous catheter to avoid peripheral extravasation injury.

### Q6 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Critical Care

An 80 kg patient in septic shock is prescribed noradrenaline (norepinephrine) at 0.1 micrograms/kg/minute to maintain a MAP ≥ 65 mmHg. The infusion has been prepared as noradrenaline 4 mg in 50 mL sodium chloride 0.9%.

**Calculate the infusion rate in mL/hour.**

UNIT: mL/hour
ANSWER: 6
TOLERANCE: 0.2
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the dose per minute = 80 kg × 0.1 mcg/kg/min = 8 mcg/min.
Step 2: Convert to hourly dose = 8 mcg/min × 60 min/h = 480 mcg/h = 0.48 mg/h.
Step 3: Calculate the concentration = 4 mg ÷ 50 mL = 0.08 mg/mL.
Step 4: Calculate the rate = 0.48 mg/h ÷ 0.08 mg/mL = **6 mL/h**.

> Noradrenaline is the first-line vasopressor in septic shock (Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines). It must be administered centrally due to its potent vasoconstrictive effect. Usual range: 0.01–3 mcg/kg/min. ECG monitoring is required; extravasation causes tissue necrosis — treat immediately with phentolamine if it occurs.

### Q7 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Cardiology

A 78 kg patient with acute heart failure is receiving dobutamine via syringe driver. The infusion contains dobutamine 250 mg in 50 mL glucose 5%, running at 9.36 mL/hour.

**Calculate the dose of dobutamine the patient is currently receiving, in micrograms/kg/minute.**

UNIT: micrograms/kg/minute
ANSWER: 10
TOLERANCE: 0.2
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the concentration = 250 mg ÷ 50 mL = 5 mg/mL.
Step 2: Calculate the hourly dose = 9.36 mL/h × 5 mg/mL = 46.8 mg/h = 46,800 mcg/h.
Step 3: Convert to per-minute dose = 46,800 mcg/h ÷ 60 min/h = 780 mcg/min.
Step 4: Calculate the weight-based dose = 780 mcg/min ÷ 78 kg = **10 mcg/kg/min**.

> Dobutamine is a beta-1 selective inotrope used in acute heart failure. Typical dose range: 2.5–20 mcg/kg/min. Adverse effects include tachycardia, arrhythmias, and hypotension. It should not be used in hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy. Tolerance develops with prolonged infusions > 72 hours.

### Q8 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Palliative Care

A patient receiving palliative care has a subcutaneous morphine sulfate infusion running via syringe driver. The syringe contains morphine sulfate 50 mg made up to 50 mL with sodium chloride 0.9%. The syringe driver is running at 4 mL/hour.

**Calculate the dose of morphine the patient is currently receiving, in mg/hour.**

UNIT: mg/hour
ANSWER: 4
TOLERANCE: 0.1
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the concentration = 50 mg ÷ 50 mL = 1 mg/mL.
Step 2: Calculate the hourly dose = 4 mL/h × 1 mg/mL = **4 mg/h**.

> A syringe driver delivers a continuous SC infusion over 24 hours. To calculate the 24-hour dose: 4 mg/h × 24 h = 96 mg/24 h. This is significantly higher than the original 50 mg/24 h prescription — the rate should be urgently reviewed. Breakthrough doses are typically 1/6 of the total daily opioid dose.

### Q9 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Neurology

A 75 kg patient is admitted following a traumatic head injury with new-onset seizures. Phenytoin 18 mg/kg is prescribed by IV infusion. Phenytoin must not be administered faster than 50 mg/minute.

**Calculate the minimum time, in minutes, over which the phenytoin infusion should be given.**

UNIT: minutes
ANSWER: 27
TOLERANCE: 0.5
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the total dose = 75 kg × 18 mg/kg = 1350 mg.
Step 2: Calculate the minimum infusion time = 1350 mg ÷ 50 mg/min = **27 minutes**.

> Phenytoin must be infused slowly (max 50 mg/min in adults) due to the risk of cardiac arrhythmias and hypotension. ECG and blood pressure monitoring is mandatory throughout. It must only be diluted in sodium chloride 0.9% (precipitates in glucose solutions). Do not use if the patient is already taking oral phenytoin/fosphenytoin — check drug history first.

### Q10 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | General Medicine

A 66-year-old woman with severe hypokalaemia requires 60 mmol of potassium chloride administered intravenously via a peripheral venous cannula. The maximum safe peripheral infusion rate is 10 mmol/hour.

**Calculate the minimum time, in hours, over which the potassium must be infused.**

UNIT: hours
ANSWER: 6
TOLERANCE: 0.1
WORKING:
Step 1: Identify the total dose required = 60 mmol.
Step 2: Identify the maximum peripheral infusion rate = 10 mmol/h.
Step 3: Calculate the minimum infusion time = 60 mmol ÷ 10 mmol/h = **6 hours**.

> NPSA guidance: potassium chloride must never be given as an IV bolus. The safe maximum rate via a peripheral line is 10 mmol/h at a concentration ≤ 40 mmol/L; up to 20 mmol/h via a central line with continuous ECG monitoring. The patient should be on a cardiac monitor throughout. Recheck potassium levels after each 40 mmol infused.

### Q11 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Infectious Disease

A 68-year-old woman with a wound infection is prescribed vancomycin 1.5 g IV over at least 90 minutes. Before administration, vancomycin must be diluted in sodium chloride 0.9% to a concentration not exceeding 5 mg/mL.

**Calculate the minimum volume, in mL, to which the dose should be diluted.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 300
TOLERANCE: 5
WORKING:
Step 1: Convert dose to milligrams = 1.5 g = 1500 mg.
Step 2: Apply the maximum concentration limit = 1500 mg ÷ 5 mg/mL = **300 mL**.

> Vancomycin must be infused slowly to prevent Red Man Syndrome (flushing, rash, hypotension) — this is a rate-related, not IgE-mediated, reaction. Pretreatment with antihistamine and slowing the infusion rate resolves it. Therapeutic drug monitoring (trough levels or AUC-guided dosing) is essential. Target trough (pre-dose): 10–15 mg/L (mild infection) or 15–20 mg/L (serious infection).

### Q12 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Renal Medicine

A 1000 mL bag of sodium chloride 0.9% requires potassium supplementation to achieve a final concentration of 40 mmol/L. Potassium chloride concentrate 15% w/v (equivalent to 2 mmol/mL) is available.

**Calculate the volume of potassium chloride concentrate, in mL, that should be added to the bag.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 20
TOLERANCE: 0.5
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the total potassium required = 40 mmol/L × 1 L = 40 mmol.
Step 2: Calculate the volume of concentrate required = 40 mmol ÷ 2 mmol/mL = **20 mL**.

> The NPSA mandates that potassium chloride concentrate (≥ 10%) must be stored separately from other IV medications and should only be added by trained staff. The final bag must be clearly labelled with the total potassium content. Concentrated KCl must never be given undiluted; it causes cardiac arrest. Compatibility should be verified — never add KCl to blood products.

### Q13 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Paediatrics

A 3.5 kg neonate on the neonatal unit requires propranolol hydrochloride 500 micrograms/kg every 8 hours (three times daily) for neonatal hyperthyroidism. Propranolol oral solution 1 mg/mL is available.

**Calculate the total volume of propranolol oral solution required each day, in mL. Give your answer to 1 decimal place.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 5.3
TOLERANCE: 0.05
WORKING:
Step 1: Convert the dose to milligrams = 500 micrograms/kg = 0.5 mg/kg.
Step 2: Calculate the single dose = 3.5 kg × 0.5 mg/kg = 1.75 mg.
Step 3: Calculate the volume per dose = 1.75 mg ÷ 1 mg/mL = 1.75 mL.
Step 4: Calculate the total daily volume = 1.75 mL × 3 doses = 5.25 mL/day → rounded to 1 d.p. = **5.3 mL/day**.

> Propranolol is used in neonatal thyrotoxicosis to control autonomic and adrenergic symptoms (tachycardia, tremor, jitteriness) while awaiting definitive treatment with carbimazole or propylthiouracil in the mother/neonate. Monitor heart rate carefully; bradycardia and hypoglycaemia are the main adverse effects in neonates.

### Q14 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Paediatrics

An 18 kg child with an acute exacerbation of tonsillitis is prescribed amoxicillin 40 mg/kg/day in 3 divided doses. The available suspension is amoxicillin 250 mg/5 mL.

**Calculate the volume of suspension to give per dose, in mL.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 4.8
TOLERANCE: 0.1
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the total daily dose = 18 kg × 40 mg/kg/day = 720 mg/day.
Step 2: Calculate the dose per administration = 720 mg ÷ 3 = 240 mg per dose.
Step 3: Calculate the volume per dose = (240 mg ÷ 250 mg) × 5 mL = **4.8 mL per dose**.

> The BNFC lists amoxicillin for tonsillitis/pharyngitis at 40 mg/kg/day in 3 divided doses (max 500 mg/dose) for 5–10 days in children 1 month–17 years. The suspension strength (125, 250, or 500 mg/5 mL) must be confirmed before calculating the dose. Shake well before each use.

### Q15 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Paediatrics

A 22 kg child on the paediatric ward requires a single dose of oral morphine sulfate for postoperative pain. The prescribed dose is 0.2 mg/kg. Oramorph oral solution 10 mg/5 mL is available.

**Calculate the volume to administer, in mL.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 2.2
TOLERANCE: 0.1
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the dose = 22 kg × 0.2 mg/kg = 4.4 mg.
Step 2: Calculate the volume = (4.4 mg ÷ 10 mg) × 5 mL = **2.2 mL**.

> Paediatric oral morphine dosing: 0.2–0.4 mg/kg/dose every 4 hours (BNFC). The 10 mg/5 mL (2 mg/mL) concentration is standard; a 100 mg/5 mL concentrated solution also exists — always confirm the strength to avoid 10-fold dosing errors. Morphine is renally cleared; dose reduction is needed in renal impairment.

### Q16 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Anaesthetics

A 45 kg patient is about to undergo a minor procedure under sedation. Fentanyl citrate 2 micrograms/kg is prescribed intravenously for analgesia. The available preparation is fentanyl citrate 50 micrograms/mL solution for injection.

**Calculate the volume of fentanyl citrate solution to draw up, in mL.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 1.8
TOLERANCE: 0.05
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the dose = 45 kg × 2 mcg/kg = 90 mcg.
Step 2: Calculate the volume = 90 mcg ÷ 50 mcg/mL = **1.8 mL**.

> Fentanyl is approximately 100 times more potent than morphine. Onset when given IV is 1–2 minutes; duration 30–60 minutes. Risk of chest wall rigidity ('wooden chest syndrome') with rapid IV injection at higher doses. Respiratory depression is the primary risk — resuscitation equipment and naloxone must be available immediately.

### Q17 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Endocrinology

A patient with hypoparathyroidism is currently prescribed alfacalcidol 1.5 micrograms orally once daily. He is switched to alfacalcidol oral drops for ease of swallowing. The oral drops are available as 2 micrograms/mL; each drop contains 0.25 micrograms of alfacalcidol.

**How many drops should the patient receive per dose?**

UNIT: drops
ANSWER: 6
TOLERANCE: 0
WORKING:
Step 1: Identify the dose required = 1.5 micrograms.
Step 2: Identify the amount per drop = 0.25 micrograms/drop.
Step 3: Calculate the number of drops = 1.5 mcg ÷ 0.25 mcg/drop = **6 drops**.

> Alfacalcidol is an activated metabolite of vitamin D used in hypoparathyroidism, renal osteodystrophy, and hypocalcaemia. When switching between formulations, confirm the per-drop concentration as preparations differ. Monitoring: serum calcium every 1–2 weeks during dose titration; aim for low-normal calcium (2.0–2.2 mmol/L) to avoid hypercalciuria and nephrocalcinosis.

### Q18 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Endocrinology

An unconscious patient in myxoedema coma requires intravenous levothyroxine sodium 1.5 mg. The available preparation is levothyroxine sodium 500 micrograms/mL solution for injection.

**Calculate the volume to administer, in mL.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 3
TOLERANCE: 0.1
WORKING:
Step 1: Convert the dose to micrograms = 1.5 mg = 1500 micrograms.
Step 2: Calculate the volume = 1500 mcg ÷ 500 mcg/mL = **3 mL**.

> Myxoedema coma is a life-threatening emergency. IV levothyroxine is given slowly (300–500 mcg loading dose, then 50–100 mcg/day) alongside IV hydrocortisone 100 mg 8-hourly to prevent precipitating an adrenal crisis. Simultaneously treat hypothermia, hypoglycaemia, hypoventilation, and any precipitating infection.

### Q19 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Hepatology

An 80 kg patient is admitted with spontaneous bacterial peritonitis. To prevent hepatorenal syndrome, albumin 1.5 g/kg is prescribed on day 1. Human albumin solution 20% is available.

**Calculate the volume of 20% albumin solution required, in mL.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 600
TOLERANCE: 5
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the total albumin dose = 80 kg × 1.5 g/kg = 120 g.
Step 2: Recall that albumin 20% contains 20 g per 100 mL (20% w/v = 20 g/100 mL).
Step 3: Calculate the volume required = (120 g ÷ 20 g) × 100 mL = **600 mL**.

> In spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, EASL guidelines recommend albumin 1.5 g/kg on day 1 and 1 g/kg on day 3, in addition to antibiotics (e.g. cefotaxime), to reduce the risk of hepatorenal syndrome and mortality. Human albumin 20% is preferred as a volume-efficient preparation; 4.5% albumin would require much larger volumes.

### Q20 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | General Medicine

A patient with severe hypoglycaemia who cannot swallow receives 500 mL of glucose 10% intravenously.

**Calculate how many grams of glucose are delivered in this infusion.**

UNIT: grams
ANSWER: 50
TOLERANCE: 0
WORKING:
Step 1: Recall that glucose 10% w/v = 10 g per 100 mL.
Step 2: Calculate the total glucose = (10 g ÷ 100 mL) × 500 mL = **50 g**.

> Glucose 10% delivers 15–20 g per 150–200 mL bolus, which is appropriate for treating hypoglycaemia in an unconscious patient. Glucose 50% (25 g per 50 mL) is an alternative but is hypertonic and irritant to veins — pH and osmolality risks mean it must be given via a large or central vein. Glucose 5% (2.5 g/50 mL) is too weak to be relied upon for treating hypoglycaemia.

### Q21 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Cardiology

A patient with severe symptomatic hyperkalaemia has an ECG showing peaked T-waves. Calcium gluconate 10% 30 mL is prescribed intravenously to stabilise the myocardium.

**Calculate the dose of calcium gluconate being administered, in grams.**

UNIT: grams
ANSWER: 3
TOLERANCE: 0
WORKING:
Step 1: Recall that calcium gluconate 10% w/v = 10 g per 100 mL.
Step 2: Calculate the dose = (10 g ÷ 100 mL) × 30 mL = **3 g**.

> Calcium gluconate (10%) 30 mL (= 3 g) is the first-line treatment for hyperkalaemia with ECG changes — it stabilises the myocardium but does NOT lower the serum potassium. It acts within minutes. MHRA advice: use with continuous ECG monitoring. Follow with insulin/dextrose (e.g. Actrapid 10 units IV + 25 g glucose) to shift K⁺ intracellularly, then a cation-exchange resin or dialysis to remove K⁺ from the body.

### Q22 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | General Medicine

A 54-year-old man is being resuscitated with IV fluids and potassium replacement. The following sequential bags are prescribed without interruption:

| Infusion fluid | Volume | KCl content | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sodium chloride 0.9%/KCl 0.15% | 500 mL | 20 mmol/L | 1 hour |
| Sodium chloride 0.9%/KCl 0.3% | 1000 mL | 40 mmol/L | 4 hours |

Blood tests are planned 4 hours after the infusions commence.

**Calculate the total dose of potassium (mmol) that will have been administered by the time the blood tests are taken.**

UNIT: mmol
ANSWER: 40
TOLERANCE: 1
WORKING:
Step 1: Potassium from Bag 1 (runs for 1 hour; completes before the 4-hour mark):
  Volume = 0.5 L; K⁺ = 0.5 L × 20 mmol/L = 10 mmol.
Step 2: Potassium from Bag 2 (starts at 1 hour; 3 hours of infusion remain within the 4-hour window):
  Rate = 1000 mL ÷ 4 h = 250 mL/h; volume infused in 3 hours = 250 mL/h × 3 h = 750 mL.
  K⁺ = 0.75 L × 40 mmol/L = 30 mmol.
Step 3: Total potassium = 10 + 30 = **40 mmol**.

> This question tests the ability to work out partial infusion volumes within a time window. Identify when each bag starts and finishes, then calculate the volume delivered from any bag still running at the end of the window. Maximum peripheral potassium infusion rate: 10 mmol/h. A higher rate requires central access and continuous ECG monitoring.

### Q23 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Palliative Care

A patient being discharged to a hospice requires a 3-day supply of subcutaneous morphine sulfate: a continuous background infusion at 30 mg/24 hours and a breakthrough dose of 5 mg up to a maximum of 4 times per day. Morphine sulfate is available as 10 mg/mL ampoules. Partially used ampoules must be discarded.

**Calculate the minimum number of ampoules that should be supplied.**

UNIT: ampoules
ANSWER: 15
TOLERANCE: 0
WORKING:
Step 1: Background infusion over 3 days = 30 mg/day × 3 days = 90 mg.
  Ampoules required = 90 mg ÷ 10 mg/ampoule = 9 ampoules.
Step 2: Maximum breakthrough doses over 3 days = 5 mg × 4 doses/day × 3 days = 60 mg.
  Ampoules required = 60 mg ÷ 10 mg/ampoule = 6 ampoules.
Step 3: Total ampoules = 9 + 6 = **15 ampoules**.

> Discharge prescriptions for controlled drugs must include the total quantity in both figures and words. All doses fit exactly into whole ampoules here, but if any remainder existed, the number must be rounded up as partial ampoules must be discarded. Breakthrough doses should be approximately 1/6 of the total 24-hour oral morphine equivalent. The 30 mg SC/24 h background dose is equivalent to 60 mg oral morphine/24 h.

### Q24 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Infectious Disease

A 34-year-old woman with bacterial meningitis is to be treated with benzylpenicillin 1.2 g intravenously every 4 hours for 5 days. Benzylpenicillin is supplied as 600 mg vials (dry powder for reconstitution).

**Calculate the total number of vials required for the full course.**

UNIT: vials
ANSWER: 60
TOLERANCE: 0
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the number of doses per day = 24 hours ÷ 4 hours = 6 doses/day.
Step 2: Calculate the total number of doses = 6 doses/day × 5 days = 30 doses.
Step 3: Calculate vials per dose = 1200 mg ÷ 600 mg/vial = 2 vials/dose.
Step 4: Total vials = 30 doses × 2 vials/dose = **60 vials**.

> Benzylpenicillin is the antibiotic of choice for bacterial meningitis caused by Neisseria meningitidis or Streptococcus pneumoniae in the UK. Dose: 2.4 g IV every 4 hours in adults (doses may be higher in severe cases). Penicillin allergy (anaphylaxis): use ceftriaxone or chloramphenicol. Always take blood cultures and lumbar puncture before starting antibiotics if this will not delay treatment by more than 30 minutes.

### Q25 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Surgery

A 35 kg child with von Willebrand disease requires treatment with desmopressin 400 nanograms/kg IV prior to surgery to reduce bleeding risk. The desmopressin is to be diluted to a total volume of 50 mL with sodium chloride 0.9% and infused over 20 minutes using a syringe driver.

Desmopressin is supplied in 1 mL ampoules containing 4 micrograms/mL.

**Calculate the volume of desmopressin solution for injection that should be added to the syringe, in mL.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 3.5
TOLERANCE: 0.1
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the total dose = 400 nanograms/kg × 35 kg = 14,000 nanograms.
Step 2: Convert to micrograms = 14,000 nanograms ÷ 1000 = 14 micrograms.
Step 3: Calculate the volume = 14 mcg ÷ 4 mcg/mL = **3.5 mL**.

> Desmopressin (DDAVP) raises plasma von Willebrand factor and factor VIII acutely (onset 30–60 minutes, duration 4–8 hours) and is useful for minor surgical procedures in mild von Willebrand disease type 1 and mild haemophilia A. It is ineffective in type 3 vWD. Adverse effects: flushing, headache, and — particularly in children — hyponatraemia from water retention; fluid restriction and electrolyte monitoring are essential.

### Q26 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Renal Medicine

A 55 kg woman with pyelonephritis requires once-daily IV gentamicin dosed at 5 mg/kg. Hospital protocol states that the calculated dose should be rounded to the nearest 40 mg before administration. Gentamicin is available as 40 mg/mL solution for injection.

**Calculate the volume of gentamicin to draw up for the initial dose, in mL.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 7
TOLERANCE: 0
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the calculated dose = 55 kg × 5 mg/kg = 275 mg.
Step 2: Round to the nearest 40 mg multiple:
  — 240 mg is 35 mg below 275 mg.
  — 280 mg is only 5 mg above 275 mg.
  → Round UP to **280 mg**.
Step 3: Calculate the volume = 280 mg ÷ 40 mg/mL = **7 mL**.

> Extended-interval (once-daily) gentamicin (5–7 mg/kg) achieves concentration-dependent bactericidal activity and allows sufficient off-time to minimise nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity compared with traditional three-times-daily dosing. Monitor pre-dose (trough) levels from dose 2 onwards (target < 1 mg/L). Adjust dose interval in renal impairment; avoid in pregnancy if possible.

### Q27 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Surgery

A postoperative patient (63 kg) requires a heparin infusion at 18 units/kg/hour for management of a deep vein thrombosis. Heparin is available as 1000 units/mL solution in vials.

**Calculate the infusion rate in mL/hour. Give your answer to 1 decimal place.**

UNIT: mL/hour
ANSWER: 1.1
TOLERANCE: 0
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the hourly dose = 63 kg × 18 units/kg/h = 1134 units/h.
Step 2: Calculate the rate = 1134 units/h ÷ 1000 units/mL = 1.134 mL/h.
Step 3: Round to 1 decimal place = **1.1 mL/h**.

> When rounding infusion rates, always round to the precision that the syringe driver can display (usually 0.1 mL/h). In this case 1.134 rounds DOWN to 1.1 — slight under-dosing is safer than over-dosing with anticoagulants. Recheck APTT 6 hours after starting and adjust the rate using the local heparin nomogram.

### Q28 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Emergency Medicine

An 80 kg patient presents to the emergency department with a staggered paracetamol overdose and is commenced on acetylcysteine. One hour after the second infusion starts, the third (and final) infusion is required: acetylcysteine 100 mg/kg is added to 1000 mL glucose 5% and infused over 16 hours.

Acetylcysteine concentrate for IV infusion is available in 10 mL ampoules containing 200 mg/mL.

**Calculate the infusion rate, in mL/hour, at which the third acetylcysteine bag should be delivered.**

UNIT: mL/hour
ANSWER: 65
TOLERANCE: 0.5
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the acetylcysteine dose = 80 kg × 100 mg/kg = 8000 mg.
Step 2: Calculate the volume of concentrate required = 8000 mg ÷ 200 mg/mL = 40 mL.
Step 3: Calculate the total infusion volume = 1000 mL (diluent) + 40 mL (concentrate) = 1040 mL.
Step 4: Calculate the infusion rate = 1040 mL ÷ 16 hours = **65 mL/hour**.

> The Prescott regimen for acetylcysteine consists of three sequential IV infusions:
> 1st: 150 mg/kg in 200 mL glucose 5% over 1 hour.
> 2nd: 50 mg/kg in 500 mL glucose 5% over 4 hours.
> 3rd: 100 mg/kg in 1000 mL glucose 5% over 16 hours.
> The volume of the drug concentrate must always be added to the total before calculating the infusion rate — a common source of calculation error. Anaphylactoid reactions (flushing, urticaria, bronchospasm) occur in ~15% of patients; slow the infusion and treat with chlorphenamine if they occur.

### Q29 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Palliative Care

A patient requires diamorphine 30 mg/24 hours via a subcutaneous syringe driver. Diamorphine hydrochloride is available as 10 mg/mL solution. The syringe driver should be made up to a total volume of 24 mL with water for injection (WFI).

**Calculate the volume of water for injection, in mL, that should be added to the syringe.**

UNIT: mL
ANSWER: 21
TOLERANCE: 0
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate the volume of diamorphine solution required = 30 mg ÷ 10 mg/mL = 3 mL.
Step 2: Calculate the volume of WFI to add = 24 mL (total) − 3 mL (diamorphine) = **21 mL**.

> Diamorphine (heroin) is the preferred opioid for syringe drivers in UK palliative care because of its high water solubility (allowing small volumes) and compatibility with common co-medications (e.g. midazolam, levomepromazine, cyclizine). Oral morphine : SC diamorphine conversion ratio ≈ 3:1 (e.g. oral morphine 90 mg/day → diamorphine 30 mg/24 h SC). Water for injection is the preferred diluent to minimise precipitation when mixing drugs.

### Q30 | CALCULATION | Calculation Skills | Emergency Medicine

A 27-year-old man is brought to the emergency department with suspected opioid overdose. He received naloxone hydrochloride 400 micrograms IV, which resulted in satisfactory respiratory effort for over 15 minutes. He subsequently became drowsy again and now requires a continuous IV infusion of naloxone at a rate that delivers 60% of the initial resuscitative dose per hour.

An infusion containing naloxone hydrochloride 2 mg made up to 500 mL with glucose 5% has been prepared.

**Calculate the volume of naloxone infusion that should be administered each hour, in mL.**

UNIT: mL/hour
ANSWER: 60
TOLERANCE: 2
WORKING:
Step 1: Calculate 60% of the resuscitative dose = 60% × 400 mcg = 240 mcg/hour.
Step 2: Calculate the concentration of the infusion = 2 mg ÷ 500 mL = 0.004 mg/mL = 4 mcg/mL.
Step 3: Calculate the infusion volume per hour = 240 mcg/h ÷ 4 mcg/mL = **60 mL/hour**.

> Naloxone has a shorter duration of action (30–90 minutes) than most opioids; re-narcotisation is common after the initial bolus wears off. The 60% infusion rule maintains steady-state reversal without full antagonism. The infusion rate may need to be adjusted based on clinical response (respiratory rate, GCS). For long-acting opioids or sustained-release preparations, the infusion may need to run for many hours. Always monitor for acute opioid withdrawal (agitation, tachycardia, hypertension, pulmonary oedema).
