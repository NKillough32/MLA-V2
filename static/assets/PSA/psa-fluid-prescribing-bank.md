### Q1 | PRESCRIBING | Fluid Prescribing | Emergency Medicine

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 72-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department by ambulance. **PMH.** Recurrent UTIs, type 2 diabetes, hypertension. **DH.** Metformin hydrochloride 500 mg PO twice daily, ramipril 5 mg PO daily, trimethoprim 200 mg PO nightly (long-term prophylaxis).

**On examination**
Temperature 38.9°C, HR 122/min and rhythm regular, BP 78/46 mmHg, RR 24/min, O_2 sat 94% breathing air. Peripherally shut down, capillary refill 4 seconds, JVP not visible. Abdomen soft. GCS 14.

**Investigations**
Na^+ 138 mmol/L (137–144), K^+ 4.2 mmol/L (3.5–5.3), U 14.4 mmol/L (2.5–7.0), Cr 168 µmol/L (60–110), lactate 4.1 mmol/L (<2.0), capillary glucose 9.8 mmol/L.

She has intravenous access. Metformin is withheld.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE IV fluid that is *most appropriate* as the *immediate* next step.
*(use the 'hospital IV fluid' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**
> This patient has septic shock (sepsis + hypotension + elevated lactate). The Surviving Sepsis Campaign recommends administering 30 mL/kg of intravenous crystalloid within the first 3 hours. The initial bolus is commonly given as a rapid 500 mL challenge. Sodium chloride 0.9% is the recommended crystalloid for resuscitation in sepsis per UK guidance. Balanced crystalloids (Hartmann's, Plasma-Lyte) are acceptable alternatives. Glucose-containing solutions are not appropriate for emergency resuscitation as they do not provide adequate volume replacement.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> 500 mL intravenous over 10–15 minutes as a bolus. Reassess after each fluid challenge (HR, BP, urine output, lactate). Further boluses may be required depending on response.

### Q2 | PRESCRIBING | Fluid Prescribing | Emergency Medicine

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 48-year-old man presents to the emergency department with a 6-hour history of haematemesis. He has vomited approximately 700 mL of bright red blood. He has not passed any stools today. **PMH.** Peptic ulcer disease, alcohol dependency. **DH.** Nil regular.

**On examination**
Temperature 36.8°C, HR 128/min and rhythm regular, BP 88/54 mmHg, RR 20/min, O_2 sat 98% breathing air. Pale, diaphoretic. Abdomen soft, epigastric tenderness. Rectal examination deferred.

**Investigations**
Hb 79 g/L (130–180), platelets 96 ×10^9/L (150–400), INR 1.3.

Urgent upper GI endoscopy is arranged. Activated major haemorrhage protocol. Two large-bore cannulae are sited.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE IV fluid to bridge the patient *until blood products are available*.
*(use the 'hospital IV fluid' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**
> In acute haemorrhage, the priority is restoration of circulating volume. Crystalloid (sodium chloride 0.9% or Hartmann's) is used as an immediate bridge while blood is being cross-matched. Each 500 mL bolus should be re-assessed for response. Avoid glucose-containing solutions — they provide no volume benefit for intravascular haemorrhage. Target permissive hypotension (MAP 50 mmHg) in penetrating trauma until surgical haemostasis; in GI bleeding, target BP sufficient to maintain organ perfusion. Blood products (red cells, FFP, platelets in 1:1:1 ratio) are the definitive treatment.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> 500 mL intravenous over 10–15 minutes once only. Reassess and repeat as needed while awaiting blood products.

**Optimal answers**

### Q3 | PRESCRIBING | Fluid Prescribing | Emergency Medicine

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 23-year-old woman with type 1 diabetes is brought to the emergency department by a friend. She has been vomiting for 48 hours and has not taken her insulin. **PMH.** Type 1 diabetes mellitus. **DH.** Insulin aspart (NovoRapid^®) SC at mealtimes, insulin detemir (Levemir^®) 18 units SC nightly.

**On examination**
Temperature 36.5°C, HR 115/min and rhythm regular, BP 86/58 mmHg, RR 28/min (Kussmaul breathing), O_2 sat 97% breathing air. Dry mucous membranes. Capillary refill 3 seconds.

**Investigations**
Na^+ 132 mmol/L (137–144), K^+ 5.8 mmol/L (3.5–5.3), U 20.1 mmol/L (2.5–7.0), Cr 162 µmol/L (60–110), random capillary glucose 28.4 mmol/L, capillary ketones 5.2 mmol/L (< 0.6).
Venous blood gas: pH 7.11 (7.35–7.45), bicarbonate 8 mmol/L (21–29).

The DKA care pathway is activated. Insulin infusion will be commenced once fluid resuscitation is underway.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE IV fluid for the *immediate* resuscitation step.
*(use the 'hospital IV fluid' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**
> Sodium chloride 0.9% is the recommended first-line fluid for DKA resuscitation — NOT glucose-containing solutions. The osmotic diuresis of DKA causes profound depletion of water, sodium, potassium, phosphate and bicarbonate. Hypotonic fluids risk cerebral oedema (especially in children). Balanced crystalloids may be used once initial resuscitation is complete. Insulin infusion should not be started until the potassium is ≥ 3.5 mmol/L to avoid fatal hypokalaemia (despite the K^+ appearing elevated here due to acidosis — intracellular K^+ is profoundly depleted).

**Dose / route / frequency**
> Per JBDS DKA guidelines: if SBP < 90 mmHg, give 500 mL sodium chloride 0.9% over 10–15 minutes as a bolus; repeat if no improvement. Following resuscitation, run at 1000 mL over 1 hour, then reduce the rate progressively.

**Optimal answers**

DRUG_OPTION: sodium chloride 0.9% solution
  DOSE: 500 mL intravenous over 10 minutes [10/10]
  DOSE: 500 mL intravenous over 15 minutes [10/10]
  DOSE: 500 mL intravenous over 20 minutes [7/10]
  DOSE: 1000 mL intravenous over 60 minutes [5/10]
  DOSE: 500 mL intravenous over 60 minutes [0/10]

### Q4 | PRESCRIBING | Fluid Prescribing | Emergency Medicine

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 55-year-old man is transferred to the emergency department from a care home. He has been unable to tolerate any oral fluids for 5 days following a gastroenteritis outbreak at the home. **PMH.** Stroke (no residual deficit), benign prostatic hypertrophy. **DH.** Finasteride 5 mg PO daily, tamsulosin hydrochloride m/r 400 micrograms PO daily.

**On examination**
Temperature 37.3°C, HR 118/min and rhythm regular, BP 84/52 mmHg, RR 20/min, O_2 sat 96% breathing air. Sunken eyes, dry mouth, markedly reduced skin turgor. Cold peripheries, capillary refill 4 seconds.

**Investigations**
Na^+ 152 mmol/L (137–144), K^+ 3.2 mmol/L (3.5–5.3), U 26.1 mmol/L (2.5–7.0), Cr 247 µmol/L (60–110), Hct 0.55 (0.37–0.47).

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE IV fluid for *immediate* resuscitation.
*(use the 'hospital IV fluid' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**
> Despite the hypernatraemia, this patient is haemodynamically compromised and requires urgent volume replacement. Emergency resuscitation with sodium chloride 0.9% takes priority over gradual correction of the hypernatraemia; the sodium concentration of the infused fluid is actually lower than the patient's serum sodium, so careful correction will occur. Do not use hypotonic fluids (e.g. glucose 5%) in this context — rapid shifts of free water risk cerebral oedema. Once haemodynamically stable, the remaining fluid deficit and hypernatraemia should be corrected slowly over 48–72 hours (no faster than 10 mmol/L per 24 hours).

**Dose / route / frequency**
> 500 mL sodium chloride 0.9% intravenous over 10–15 minutes. Reassess HR and BP after each bolus. May need further challenges. Once resuscitation complete, change to measured deficit replacement over 48–72 hours.

**Optimal answers**

DRUG_OPTION: sodium chloride 0.9% solution
  DOSE: 500 mL intravenous over 10 minutes [10/10]
  DOSE: 500 mL intravenous over 15 minutes [10/10]
  DOSE: 500 mL intravenous over 20 minutes [7/10]
  DOSE: 500 mL intravenous over 30 minutes [5/10]
  DOSE: 500 mL intravenous over 60 minutes [0/10]

### Q5 | PRESCRIBING | Fluid Prescribing | Endocrinology

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 34-year-old woman with type 1 diabetes is found unresponsive in the ED waiting room. Her friend reports she had symptoms of a 'hypo' 30 minutes ago. Glucagon 1 mg IM was administered by a nurse 20 minutes ago but she has remained unresponsive. Intravenous access has been established. **PMH.** Type 1 diabetes mellitus. **DH.** Insulin aspart (NovoRapid^®) SC mealtimes, insulin glargine (Lantus^®) 22 units SC nightly.

**On examination**
Unresponsive to voice, responds only to pain. Temperature 37.1°C, HR 108/min and rhythm regular, BP 142/88 mmHg, RR 20/min, O_2 sat 97% breathing air. Clammy.

**Investigations**
Random capillary glucose 1.6 mmol/L.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE IV fluid to treat this patient's condition.
*(use the 'hospital IV fluid' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**
> Glucose 20% 100 mL or glucose 10% 150–200 mL are the first-line IV treatments for severe hypoglycaemia with loss of consciousness. Glucagon has already been given and has failed. Glucose 5% provides insufficient glucose per volume and should NOT be used. Glucose 50% is hypertonic and causes serious chemical venous burns if it extravasates — it must only be given via a large or central vein and is NOT acceptable via a peripheral cannula. Insulin long-acting (glargine) will continue to act; treat the hypoglycaemia, then establish oral carbohydrate intake to prevent recurrence.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> Glucose 20%: 100 mL IV over 15 minutes via a large peripheral vein. Re-check glucose every 10–15 minutes and repeat if BG remains < 4 mmol/L. Once conscious, provide long-acting oral carbohydrate. Do not give as a bolus — the hyperosmolar solution requires dilution by blood flow to avoid thrombophlebitis.

### Q6 | PRESCRIBING | Fluid Prescribing | General Medicine

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 68-year-old man with type 2 diabetes is found confused on the medical ward. He was admitted 2 days ago with a hip fracture and has been nil by mouth since midnight. His regular medications include gliclazide 40 mg PO twice daily, which was continued on the ward. A nurse notes he is sweating profusely and is difficult to rouse.

**On examination**
Temperature 36.6°C, HR 96/min and rhythm regular, BP 138/82 mmHg, RR 16/min, O_2 sat 97% breathing air. Glasgow coma score 9/15. IV access is in situ.

**Investigations**
Random capillary glucose 1.8 mmol/L.

Glucagon 1 mg IM is not available on the ward.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE IV fluid to treat this patient's hypoglycaemia.
*(use the 'hospital IV fluid' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**
> Glucose 20% 100 mL IV or glucose 10% 150–200 mL IV are the optimal treatments. Gliclazide (a sulphonylurea) causes hypoglycaemia by stimulating insulin secretion independently of blood glucose; once glucose is given, sulphonylurea-induced hypoglycaemia is likely to recur — the patient will need a prolonged glucose infusion and re-checking of BG every 15–30 minutes. Gliclazide should be withheld until the patient can eat and its use reviewed given renal function and fasting status.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> Glucose 20% 100 mL IV over 15 minutes. Sulphonylurea-induced hypoglycaemia is prolonged — if BG does not remain ≥ 4 mmol/L after correction, commence a glucose 10% infusion at 100 mL/hour and recheck BG every 30 minutes. Consider octreotide 50 micrograms SC 8-hrly to suppress insulin secretion in refractory cases.

**Optimal answers**

  DOSE: 100 mL intravenous over 30 minutes [5/10]

### Q8 | PRESCRIBING | Fluid Prescribing | Cardiology

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 65-year-old woman is admitted to the acute medical unit following 4 days of vomiting and diarrhoea from a norovirus outbreak. **PMH.** Hypertension, type 2 diabetes. **DH.** Furosemide 40 mg PO daily, ramipril 5 mg PO daily, metformin hydrochloride 500 mg PO twice daily.

**On examination**
Temperature 37.0°C, HR 100/min and rhythm regular, BP 126/78 mmHg, RR 16/min. Peripheral access in situ.

**Investigations**
Na^+ 136 mmol/L (137–144), K^+ 2.1 mmol/L (3.5–5.3), U 12.2 mmol/L (2.5–7.0), Cr 138 µmol/L (60–110).
ECG: sinus rhythm with prominent U-waves and ST depression in lateral leads.

**Prescribing request**
Write a prescription for ONE IV fluid for the *urgent* treatment of her hypokalaemia.
*(use the 'hospital IV fluid' prescription form provided)*

**Drug choice**
> Potassium chloride infusion is required for K^+ 2.1 mmol/L with ECG changes (U-waves, ST depression). A premixed bag of sodium chloride 0.9%/potassium chloride 0.3% (40 mmol/L) provides the highest safe peripheral potassium concentration. The NPSA mandates that potassium chloride concentrate must NEVER be given undiluted — cardiac arrest will result. Via a peripheral line, the maximum safe rate is 10 mmol/h; at a concentration of 40 mmol/L (0.3%), 1000 mL must therefore run over at least 4 hours (= 250 mL/h). Continuous cardiac monitoring is required. Do NOT use glucose-containing fluids when urgently correcting K^+ — the glucose-stimulated insulin release will drive K^+ further into cells and worsen hypokalaemia.

**Dose / route / frequency**
> Sodium chloride 0.9%/potassium chloride 0.3% solution 1000 mL intravenous over 4 hours. Recheck K^+ after each litre. Continue until K^+ ≥ 3.5 mmol/L, then transition to oral potassium replacement. Address the underlying causes: furosemide and diarrhoea both cause renal and GI potassium losses.

**Optimal answers**

DRUG_OPTION: sodium chloride 0.9%/potassium chloride 0.3% solution (40 mmol/L)
  DOSE: 1000 mL intravenous over 4 hours [10/10]
  DOSE: 1000 mL intravenous over 5 hours [8/10]
  DOSE: 1000 mL intravenous over 6 hours [7/10]
  DOSE: 1000 mL intravenous over 3 hours [0/10]
  DOSE: 1000 mL intravenous over 2 hours [0/10]
  DOSE: 1000 mL intravenous over 8 hours [0/10]
  DOSE: 500 mL intravenous over 2 hours [10/10]
  DOSE: 500 mL intravenous over 1 hour [0/10]

DRUG_OPTION: sodium chloride 0.9%/potassium chloride 0.15% solution (20 mmol/L)
  DOSE: 1000 mL intravenous over 2 hours [5/10]
  DOSE: 1000 mL intravenous over 4 hours [5/10]

### Q11 | PRESCRIBING | Fluid Prescribing | Gastroenterology

DRUG_MARKS: 5
DOSE_MARKS: 5

**Case presentation**
A 44-year-old man with Crohn's disease is admitted with a step-up flare. He has had high-output diarrhoea (> 10 loose stools/day) for 2 weeks. He has been unable to maintain adequate oral intake. **PMH.** Crohn's disease (pan-colonic), type 2 diabetes. **DH.** Azathioprine 150 mg PO daily, mesalazine 800 mg PO 8-hrly, metformin hydrochloride 500 mg PO twice daily.

**On examination**
Temperature 37.8°C, HR 104/min, BP 110/68 mmHg, RR 18/min. Weight 61 kg (usual weight 68 kg). Postural drop: sitting BP 110/68, standing BP 92/58 mmHg. IV access sited. Six-day metformin hold initiated.

**Investigations**
Na^+ 130 mmol/L (137–144), K^+ 3.1 mmol/L (3.5–5.3), U 14.4 mmol/L (2.5–7.0), Cr 144 µmol/L (60–110).

CRP 86 mg/L (< 10), albumin 24 g/L (35–50).
